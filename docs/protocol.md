# Signed channel protocol

The protocol uses one casino owner for signing and house withdrawals, FIFO finalized winnings allocation, EOA signatures throughout, and beneficiary-authorized `claimTo`. Each deployment has its own database and signing history.

The governing [design constraint](../architecture.md#design-constraint) is simplicity. Clearly disclosed trust assumptions, weaker guarantees, and manual user responsibilities are acceptable tradeoffs for less protocol complexity. Stronger guarantees require an explicit product need.

The EIP-712 domain is name `HookedIn`, version `1`, chain ID, and verifying contract. The canonical typed structures and hash functions are in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts) and [contracts/HookedInCasino.sol](../contracts/HookedInCasino.sol). This is a pure prototype: signed structures and contract layouts can change without preserving older deployments or data. There are no compatibility fields or migrations. Use a fresh deployment and data after an incompatible change.

## Opening

Anyone can call `openChannel(signer)` with a positive ETH deposit, without a casino request or signature. The contract derives `channelId = keccak256(abi.encode(msg.sender, signer, msg.value))`, registers the funding account and channel key, and protects the deposit. The key must be nonzero and each funding-account/key/deposit combination is single-use. One channel may be active per funding account; use a fresh channel key for each opening. Copying another player's transaction cannot reserve that player's channel ID. Binding the deposit also prevents a reorg replacement from changing the deposit under an existing ID.

`Opening(channelId, player, signer, deposit)` is unsigned identity data checked against the contract during registration and independent recovery. The genesis checkpoint has sequence 0, balance=deposit and zero preceding/transition hashes. It supports unilateral closure without any casino response, using the ordinary fixed 24-hour challenge window.

After confirmation, the wallet authenticates with the registered channel key and submits this identity to `/api/channels/:id/activate`. Confirmation depth is two on Sepolia and one on Anvil. The service retains only authenticated, confirmed registrations. Debits and credits need no randomness. Opening and activation create nothing else: the casino names a channel's first [round](#rounds) when the wallet asks for it.

## Test coins

A channel holds one asset. `AssetId` is `'eth' | 'test'`, and `ASSETS` in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts) gives ETH and TEST, both with 18 decimals. An ETH channel is opened and settled on-chain. Test coins are the casino's own play money: nothing about a test channel ever reaches the contract.

A test channel's ID is `keccak256(abi.encode(keccak256("HOOKEDIN/TESTCOINS"), player, signer))`, whose first word is a hash rather than an address, so it can never be the ID of a channel the contract knows. Its deposit is 0. It is opened by `POST /api/channels/:id/activate` with `{opening, asset: 'test'}`: there is no chain to read and nothing to confirm, so it is open as soon as the casino knows it. The casino's test bankroll starts at `TEST_BANKROLL`, ten million test coins.

The faucet is a credit counterparty, like the fund: a claim is an ordinary credit whose details name `FAUCET_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/FAUCET")`, signed by the channel's own key. The casino pays exactly `FAUCET_AMOUNT`, 100 coins, to a test channel that holds fewer than `FAUCET_BELOW`, 10, and refuses anything else with code `not-due`. Faucet coins are newly minted, so a claim does not change the test bankroll.

Every wallet has a test channel, funded or not. A wallet that has deposited nothing plays with test coins, and its channel is filled from the faucet the moment it starts, so a guest can play at once. An account with an open ETH channel plays with its ETH unless the player chooses test coins.

The casino keeps every figure per asset: bankroll, equity, escrow, commissions, developer totals, active liabilities and channel count. `GET /api/status` and `GET /api/metrics` carry a `test` object beside the ETH figures. The [bankroll fund](#the-bankroll-fund) takes ETH only, and a test channel has nothing on-chain to close; both refuse with code `wrong-asset`. Test channels are invisible to chain observation and to a watchtower.

## Checkpoints and transitions

`Checkpoint(channelId, sequence, previousStateHash, transitionHash, balance)` is signed by the channel key and casino. It carries money and position only: randomness belongs to the [round](#rounds) each casino bet names.

`Operation(channelId, previousStateHash, sequence, kind, amount, prizes, round, seedHash, memo)` binds exact terms and position, where `prizes` is a list of `Prize(rangeStart, rangeEnd, payout)`. An executed operation advances exactly once from the preceding state. Rejection uses a jointly signed checkpoint above the attempted operation, as described below. The contract settles money and reads nothing else: every field a kind does not use must be zero, so each operation has one meaning and one encoding. A casino bet names its round, its seed hash and 1 to 64 prizes; a debit and a credit leave all three empty; a checkpoint-only proof carries the canonical empty step. Amounts, deposits and balances are below 2^128 wei.

| Kind         | Authorization | Effect                                                                                   |
| ------------ | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 casino bet | Channel key   | balance - amount + every prize holding the outcome; settled by the secret of its `round` |
| 2 debit      | Channel key   | balance - amount                                                                         |
| 3 credit     | Channel key   | balance + amount                                                                         |

`memo` is what the operation means to the wallet and the casino: the hash (`hashJSON`, keccak256 of canonical JSON) of its **details**, `{id, game?, group?, counterparty?, developerBet?}`. `id` is the hash of the wallet's own name for the operation, so an exact retry is the same operation, and an ID cannot be reused with another digest; a game's operation takes its name from the game's own ID, the asset and the game's key, so it is the same on every channel of its player (see [fast exchange](#fast-exchange)). `game` is the [key](#games) of the game that asked for a bet or a payment. `group` is a label of up to 64 characters the game gives its bets and payments, such as one hand or one match, so they are shown and found together. `counterparty` is what a debit pays into or a credit collects from: the [bankroll fund](#the-bankroll-fund), a [developer's bank](#a-developers-bank), a [developer bet](#developer-bets), the [developer earnings](#developer-earnings) or the [faucet](#test-coins). `developerBet` makes a debit a [developer bet](#developer-bets) and says how it is settled. A casino bet names its game; a debit names its game (a payment, or a developer bet), or what it pays into (an investment, a bank deposit); a credit names what it collects from; only what names a game carries a group. Every field is in one form (a checksummed address, lowercase hashes, decimal strings), so one meaning has one memo. A request carries the details whole beside the signed operation, the casino refuses details that do not hash to the memo, and both sides keep them with the evidence, where `verify-evidence` checks them against it. Commission is not part of the signed operation.

The wallet signs the exact stake and every prize. The service calculates the even commission, [the edge the bankroll does not need](../architecture.md#settled-trade-offs), using the [casino bet's Kelly condition](economics.md#a-casino-bet-is-one-wager) against current unreserved accounting bankroll, and reserves the bet's worst-case cash decrease plus commission synchronously before reading its round's secret or awaiting signing. Reservations cover every casino bet being decided in the single service process, until it commits or fails. The service makes its capacity decision before it reads the secret, so no rejection can depend on the outcome, and a casino bet it has admitted is settled whatever happens to capacity afterwards. If capacity is insufficient, it returns a signed rejection checkpoint. Commission is reported in the response to a player's casino bet and retained in the operator ledger; it is not an extra player debit or a wallet-verified fee commitment. The contract reserves no potential winnings. This is per-operation honest-operator risk admission, not collateral or a whole-hand reservation.

A casino bet's `round` is the hash of a secret and its `seedHash` the hash of a seed, and only that secret and that seed settle it. Outcome is the low 64 bits of `keccak256(abi.encode(keccak256("HOOKEDIN/OUTCOME"), seed, secret))`. **The stake is paid to enter, and every prize whose range holds the outcome pays**: `balance − amount + Σ payout` over the prizes with `rangeStart <= outcome < rangeEnd`. A prize is any non-empty range within `[0, 2^64)` with a positive payout below 2^128. Prizes may overlap, in which case they add; they need no order; an outcome in no prize pays nothing. One prize below a threshold is a coin-flip style wager, a prize per bucket is a Plinko board, a prize smaller than the stake is a partial loss, and a roulette player's chips are simply that player's prizes, overlapping where the chips do. The contract asks nothing more of a casino bet: whether it is worth taking is the casino's [admission](economics.md) decision, so covering the whole outcome space is legal and a bet that cannot lose is merely one the casino declines. The outcome depends only on the round and its seed, never on the channel or the rest of the operation, so every bet on one round and seed sees the same 64-bit value. Transition hash is `keccak256(abi.encode(hashOperation,secret))`.

An on-chain Step contains only the operation/authorization, the revealed seed and secret, and the casino signature over the derived checkpoint; the contract checks both against the hashes the casino bet signed. Evidence is either a joint checkpoint (or registered genesis), or that checkpoint plus one Step. The latter is independently usable even if the final player acknowledgment was lost. Games and their local allocations are outside the settlement protocol.

## Rounds

Every casino bet is on a **round**. The casino picks a random secret, keeps it, and names the round by its hash: `round = keccak256(secret)`. A casino bet signs that name and the hash of a **seed**, `seedHash = keccak256(seed)`, and the round's outcome is a function of the secret and the seed together. Only its one secret and that one seed can settle a casino bet that signs them, so a round needs no signature of its own; the contract checks exactly those two hashes and knows nothing else about rounds. The casino names a round before its seed is picked, so neither side can know the outcome before both are out. A round is used once: it is **revealed** when its casino bet settles or is declined, and a revealed round settles nothing more. A round is a channel's own or a developer's.

**A channel's own round.** `POST /api/channels/:id/round` names the round that channel's next casino bet settles on, `{id}`, and names the same one until it is used. The wallet picks its seed only after it has that name, signs the seed's hash into the casino bet and sends it with the seed, `{request, details, signature, acknowledgment, seed}`, to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations`. The round opens and closes with the casino bet: the reply is the result, and carries `nextRound`, the round after it, so a casino bet stays one request. A casino bet on a round that is not the channel's next one (a restored backup, a casino that lost its data) is declined like any other, and the reply names the channel's next round.

**A channel's round that settles nobody is revealed all the same.** When the casino declines a casino bet on it, the rejection carries the round's `secret`: the wallet checks it against the round the bet signed and, with the seed it picked itself, records at once what the bet would have paid. It countersigns nothing less. The one exception is a round the casino says it has lost: that rejection says `lost: true` instead, and the receipt says in words that the bet cannot be checked. A casino that declines casino bets by outcome is therefore visible to its players on the receipts themselves, without any protocol penalty.

**A developer's round** is the round of the developer's own casino bet, which the [developer bets](#developer-bets) with prizes of its games name. `POST /api/rounds` with `{asset}` names a new one in that asset every time the developer asks, and the developer keeps track of its rounds. The developer then commits the seed of its casino bet on it, `POST /api/rounds/:round/commit` with `{seedHash, signature}`, the signature being its `Commit(bytes32 round, bytes32 seedHash)`; a round takes one commitment, and the same one again is answered as it stands. Both answer with the round as `GET /api/rounds/:round` shows it to anyone, `{id, developer, asset, status, seedHash?, signature?, seed?, secret?, outcome?, casinoBet?}`: `status` is `open` until the developer's casino bet reveals it, and a revealed round shows its seed, its secret, their 64-bit outcome and that casino bet, `{game, stake, prizes, covers, signature, accepted, payout?}`. A game's page learns its round from its own server.

**Durability.** A round's secret is private service state and is not in the signing history until the round is revealed; the reveal is, with a developer's commitment. A stale database restore replays the reveal, so no secret that was ever public can settle another bet, and a round whose secret was lost is simply unknown.

## Developer bets

A casino bet settles against the bankroll in the request that places it. A **developer bet** is a bet against the game's developer instead, which the developer settles afterwards. It is a debit whose details name the game, any group, and in `developerBet` either `{round, seedHash, prizes}`, a round of the developer's, the seed hash its developer committed to it and the bet's prizes, or `{terms}`, the game's own JSON object of up to 4,096 bytes of canonical JSON whose numbers are whole, such as a pick and its odds. The reply is the signed result at once: **the bet is final**, and its stake is in the developer's [bank](#a-developers-bank). Whatever the developer pays it is owed to its player, listed by `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` under the bet's hash and collected with a credit that names it. The bet is known by that hash, the hash of the operation that placed it, which signs its terms.

**Placing one trusts the developer.** The casino takes any well-formed developer bet on a published game: there is no escrow, no deadline and no refund, the stake is the developer's at once, and only the developer can settle the bet, with `Settlement(bytes32 bet, uint256 player, uint256 casino)` signed by its key. The developer is the account that publishes the game (see [games](#games)), and proves itself with `DeveloperAccess(address developer, uint256 expiresAt)` signed by that key, sent like a channel's `Access`; the casino turns away a key whose account publishes no game before it counts against the developers' rate budget. A game's server therefore holds everything that account holds; a developer who wants their server to hold less publishes the game from an account of its own.

| The developer bet has | It is owed                                                                                                                             | Its receipt                                                                      |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `terms`               | what its developer's settlement pays: the developer's word                                                                             | `settled`                                                                        |
| `prizes`              | what its prizes pay on its round's outcome if the developer's accepted casino bet on the round covers it, and its stake back otherwise | `settled`, `returned` if it was not covered, or `shorted` if paid less than owed |

**A developer bet with prizes** is provably fair. Before it signs, the wallet reads the round the game named, checks that it is open, the developer's and in its asset, and that the developer signed its commitment, and names the round and the seed hash in the bet: **the bet's outcome is fixed before it is placed**, and neither the casino nor the developer knows it. When its betting ends, the developer places its own casino bet on the round from its bank: `POST /api/rounds/:round/casino-bet` with `{game, stake, prizes, covers, seed, signature}`, the signature being its `BankCasinoBet(bytes32 round, bytes32 game, uint256 stake, Prize[] prizes, bytes32[] covers)`. `covers` names up to 256 developer bets by hash. The casino takes it only if each is open, of this developer, in the round's asset and signed with the round's committed seed hash, the seed is the one committed, `game` is one of the developer's games, and the bank holds the stake. It is then admitted like any casino bet, before the round's secret is read. **Accepted**, its stake leaves the bank and what its prizes pay on the outcome comes back to it, and half its commission is the developer's, as for any casino bet of its game. **Declined**, it moves no money and covers nothing. Either way one signing-history record reveals the round. The round is the casino bet's ID: the same casino bet again, after a lost reply, is answered as it stands, and any other is refused with code `round-revealed`.

The round's outcome then decides what each developer bet on it is owed: **what its prizes pay if the developer's accepted casino bet covers it, and its stake back if not**. Players sign their own prizes, so only bets the developer chose before the outcome existed can be owed prizes: a developer bet placed after the casino bet, or one with prizes the developer would not back, is returned. A developer's hedge is the developer's business: the bankroll sees one casino bet with many prizes, admitted like any other.

**Settling.** The developer settles developer bets whenever it chooses, with its `Settlement`s posted together in `POST /api/developer-bets/settle` as `{settlements: [{bet, player, casino, signature}]}`, up to 256 at once: `player` is what the player is paid and `casino` what the casino is given, both from the developer's bank. The bank pays the batch whole or, if it cannot, nothing in it is settled and it is refused with code `bank-short`. A bet already settled answers with what settled it. The casino's share is its commission on a developer bet, and the developer's to choose: the casino's policy asks for about half of what the bet is expected to earn the developer, and nothing enforces it. A covered developer bet with prizes paid its commission already, on the developer's casino bet.

**Reading them.** `GET /api/developer-bets/:hash` shows a developer bet to anyone: `{bet, game, group?, asset, uname, alias, developer, stake, placedAt, status}` with its `round`, `seedHash` and `prizes` or its `terms`, and once settled its `settlement` and `settledAt`. `placedAt` is when the casino took it. `GET /api/developer-bets?game=<key>&status=open|settled&group=<group>&after=…&limit=100` is a page of a game's developer bets, of one group if it names one, up to 256 at a time, `{bets, cursor, more}`: open ones ordered by hash, and settled ones in the order they settled, durable in the signing history, so a developer that saves the cursor never misses one.

A game nobody publishes takes no developer bets, and a signed developer bet the casino does not take, for that or because its player already carried out the operation on another channel, is answered with the ordinary signed rejection, never an error the wallet would get again.

**Account discovery.** Authenticated `GET /api/channels/:id/developer-bets?status=open|settled&after=…&limit=50` returns `{bets, cursor, more}` for the channel's funding account and asset, across its channels. Each is `{bet, game, group?, asset, status, stake, collected}`, and a settled one adds `payout` and `settledAt`. `collected` means a positive payout has been credited; a zero payout needs no collection. The default limit is 50, maximum 100. Pass `cursor` as `after` while `more` is true. Open bets are ordered by hash: refresh from the beginning each time, since this is a changing set. Settled bets are ordered by the order they settled in: save the cursor and resume from it, including after an empty page. The feed includes zero payouts.

**Collect.** The wallet works out what a settled developer bet is owed before it signs the credit. It checks the developer's signature over the bet's hash and its settlement. For a bet with prizes it reads the round: it checks the seed against the seed hash and the secret against the round, both of which the bet signed, and the developer's casino bet against the developer's signature, and reads its own prizes against their outcome. It signs a credit for what the settlement pays and nothing else, and the receipt says what the bet was owed beside it. `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` lists at most 256 payouts at a time; the wallet collects them, and the next reply lists the rest. A player who is away collects whenever their wallet next looks.

**Durability.** Developer bets, developers' casino bets and settlements are in the signing history, so a restart or a stale restore loses none of them: an open developer bet stays open. A developer's round is private service state like a channel's own until it is revealed; one whose secret was lost is unknown, the developer bets that name it can never be covered, and the developer opens another.

Trust differs by kind, and the receipt says so. A developer bet with prizes has its outcome fixed before it is placed: the casino fixed the round's secret and the developer committed its seed first, and every bet names both, so **nobody can change the outcome**, and neither knows it alone before the reveal. Developer and casino together could know it in advance and cover by outcome, which each returned bet's receipt shows, with what it would have paid. What a developer bet with terms pays is the developer's word, which its signature makes attributable and nothing more; a game that wants its offer attributable too can put the developer's signed offer in the terms. And a developer bet is paid only if its developer pays: a developer can keep a stake by never settling, which the game's public record shows as a developer bet left open.

## A developer's bank

A developer's **bank** is a balance at the casino, per asset, that holds its money for developer bets: the stakes of its games' developer bets go in as they are placed, its settlements and its casino bets are paid from it, and an accepted casino bet's payout goes back in. Nothing in it is reserved, so a developer can offer odds on events far off without locking up what a win would cost; a batch of settlements the bank cannot pay in full is refused. Whether a developer can pay is [outside HookedIn](../architecture.md#settled-trade-offs): playing their game trusts them.

```text
BankStatement(address developer, string asset, uint256 sequence, uint256 balance, bytes32 cause)
Withdraw(address developer, string asset, uint256 amount, uint256 sequence)
```

`BANK_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/BANK")` is its counterparty. **Depositing** is a debit that names it, from the developer's own channel, and the response carries the casino's `BankStatement` with the deposit's hash as `cause`. **Withdrawing** starts with the developer's `Withdraw`, signed by their channel's key and posted to `POST /api/channels/:id/bank/withdraw`; `sequence` is the statement it will produce, so it works once, and asking again returns the recorded statement. The amount is owed to the developer at once, listed under `BANK_ID` and collected with a credit. `GET /api/channels/:id/bank` is the bank as it is now: every developer bet, settlement and casino bet of the developer moves its balance between statements, so the wallet reads it before it withdraws and checks each statement's signature and cause, not its arithmetic.

## Games

A game's **key** is `keccak256(abi.encode(developer, name))`: made from its developer, the account that publishes it, and the name they publish it under, and kept wherever it is served. A bet and a payment sign the game's key in their details, and the casino keeps every game's bets, commission and public record under it. Publishing records `{name, url}` in the developer's profile, from their own funded channel, with `POST /api/channels/:id/games`, and the profile lists each game as `{name, url, developer, key}`. The developer earns half of each casino bet's commission, as the casino finds the game published when the bet settles, and takes and settles the game's [developer bets](#developer-bets); a game nobody publishes earns no developer anything and takes no developer bets. The manifest at `url` names the developer too: a wallet that opens `@alias/name` refuses the game if it names another, so nobody else can publish a game somebody hosts. To move hosts, the developer publishes the game at the new URL, and it keeps its key, its history and its receipts. A game loaded straight from its manifest has the key of that manifest's developer and URL, a name no published game can have.

## The bankroll fund

Anyone with a channel can **invest in the bankroll** and later divest. An investor trusts the casino completely. A share is the casino's signed promise of a part of the bankroll: no contract holds it, the casino alone states what the bankroll is worth, and the bankroll can lose. What the protocol gives the investor is proof of what they hold, and the casino no way to deny it.

```text
ShareStatement(address holder, uint256 sequence, uint256 shares, uint256 amount, uint256 equity, uint256 totalShares, bytes32 cause)
Redeem(address holder, uint256 shares, uint256 sequence)
Fund(uint256 sequence, uint256 totalShares, uint256 houseShares, uint256 equity, uint256 overdrawn, uint256 at)
```

The fund is a counterparty: `FUND_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/BANKROLL")`. **Investing** is a debit that names it, sent to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations` like a payment. No ETH moves: the signed balance falls, so the accounting bankroll, and with it what the casino can cover, rises by the same amount. **Equity** is that bankroll before the worst cases of the casino bets being decided. The price is fixed before anything is awaited: `amount × totalShares / equity` shares, rounded down, are minted to the channel's **player address**, so a holding outlives any one channel. With the first investment the bankroll the house had already built becomes the house's own shares, one per wei. The response carries the casino's `ShareStatement`: the holder's shares afterwards, the amount, the `equity` and `totalShares` just before that fixed the price, and as `cause` the hash of the holder's signed investment. One signing-history record holds the step and the fund change. An amount too small for a share, or a bankroll with nothing left, is declined with the ordinary signed rejection.

**Divesting** starts with the holder's `Redeem`, signed by the key of an open channel of that player and posted to `POST /api/channels/:id/fund/redeem`. `sequence` is the statement it will produce, so it works once; asking again returns the recorded statement. The shares are burned for `shares × equity / totalShares`, rounded down, which leaves the bankroll at once and is owed to the player: listed by `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` under `FUND_ID` and collected with a credit for exactly that amount. A redemption larger than the unreserved bankroll is refused until the bets counting on that money settle. Minting and burning at the going price leave every other share worth what it was.

A holder's evidence is their highest-numbered statement; the casino's only answer to a smaller holding is a later `Redeem` the holder signed. The wallet checks each statement's signature, that it follows its last one, that its cause is the exact investment or redemption it signed, and that the shares and amount are what the stated price implies. It signs for redeemed money only in an amount one of its statements priced. It cannot check `equity` or `totalShares` themselves: players' balances are private, so the price is the casino's word. `GET /api/fund` returns that word signed and timestamped, a quote the casino can be held to, and `GET /api/channels/:id/fund` the player's holding with its statement.

**The owner's capital.** Everything the chain can show moves pool cash and a liability together (deposits, closures, claims), except the owner adding to the pool or withdrawing from it. At each committed observation any such change in equity is the house's: it is issued, or gives up, shares at the price just before, in a signing-history record of its own, so the price does not move. The house cannot give up shares it does not have. That excess is a loss every holder bears, and is reported as `overdrawn` from then on. While an investor's channel is open the contract still protects their original deposit, so the owner cannot withdraw it; that protection ends with the channel, and the shares remain a promise. Owner capital that moves while the service is down with its database lost is not attributed.

## Developer earnings

Half of every completed casino bet's commission accrues to its game's developer, the account that publishes the game when the bet settles. It is owed to that **address** and collected like a redemption. The casino keeps a running tally per developer address and per asset: what that address has earned, and what of it is collected. Because every casino bet signs its `game`, the casino keeps that tally per game as well, and a developer of several games sees which one earned what.

A developer sees the tally in their own channel. Whenever that address has ever earned anything, `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` carries `{source: DEVELOPER_ID, index: 0, amount, earned, collected}`, where `DEVELOPER_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/DEVELOPER")` and `amount` is `earned − collected` and may be "0".

It is collected with an ordinary channel opened from that address: a credit whose counterparty is `DEVELOPER_ID`, signed by that channel's key. There is nothing to check, so the wallet signs it by itself. The casino accepts a credit for no more than is due and refuses anything else with code `not-due`. Nothing moves on-chain and the bankroll does not change: money the casino already owed the developer becomes that developer's signed balance, which settles like any other.

The tally is the casino's word: it cannot be held to having counted every casino bet. Casino bets are private to their channels; a player's receipt shows the commission of each, and the [admission rule](economics.md) that fixes it is public.

## Fast exchange

1. Wallet saves the exact request and channel-key signature. The request carries no checkpoint signature: the casino never holds a player-signed checkpoint above a wager it has not yet decided.
2. Casino requires acknowledgment of its previous response, validates the authorization and exact terms, and serializes the channel position.
3. Casino commits either the signed result or its signature over an unchanged-balance rejection checkpoint at n+2 to its signing history and result store before sending it. Commission accrues only on an executed casino bet. A declined casino bet's round is revealed in the same record.
4. Wallet verifies the result or rejection, countersigns the new checkpoint, and saves the evidence, receipt and cleared pending operation together before returning to the game. The countersignature travels as the acknowledgment of the next request.

The rejection checkpoint retains the base balance. Its `previousStateHash` identifies the base and its `transitionHash` is the exact rejected operation digest. Nothing is settled on the declined round. Once the wallet countersigns it, the existing contract supports it without a new transition kind; until then the jointly signed base settles to the identical balance, so the casino defends with the base and loses nothing. After saving rejection n+2, the wallet can submit a new bet at n+3. The higher checkpoint can challenge an attempted settlement of the rejected n+1 wager; the player must still meet the existing challenge deadline. Because the player signs the rejection only after receiving it, a completed result at n+1 can never be superseded by a rejection the casino signs later.

The next request can carry acknowledgment. Retrying an operation ID returns the same accepted or rejected receipt; a new attempt needs a new ID. A game's operation belongs to its player, not to a channel: one its player already carried out on another channel is declined with a signed rejection that carries `used: true` and the reason "This operation was carried out on another channel", and a casino bet's round is revealed as any decline reveals it. The wallet tells the game `id-used`. Only a verified joint rejection permits the wallet to clear a rejected signed wager. An HTTP error or timeout leaves it unresolved: recover the exact request or close. Previously committed results remain recoverable during stale chain observations, even when new admission is paused. The wallet has its round before it picks each casino bet's seed.

Unsigned bankroll information in responses and `/api/metrics` is a planning hint, not an admission promise. The wallet sends the selected wager directly; the service accepts it or returns a signed rejection. Deploy the contract, wallet and service from the same prototype revision: `GET /api/config` carries `protocol` (`PROTOCOL` in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts)), the hash of every typed structure and of the rules the two sides apply alike: the operation kinds, the outcome tag, the counterparty IDs and every limit. `limits` in the same reply are those bounds, `{prizes, outcomeSpace, covers, terms, group}`, the numbers the wallet reports to a game. `developerProtocol` hashes only what a developer shares with the casino (`DeveloperAccess`, `Commit`, `Settlement`, `BankCasinoBet`, the outcome and the limits), so a change to what only wallets sign does not stop a developer's server. A wallet or a developer built against another revision stops there, before it signs anything. Every refusal of the API is `{error, code}`: the text is for people and the `code` is what a client acts on, such as `wrong-seed`, `not-covered`, `bank-short`, `round-revealed`, `unacknowledged`, `paused`, `rate-limited` or `not-found`.

## Closing

`cooperativeClose(evidence, playerCloseSignature, casinoCloseSignature)` uses `Close(channelId,stateHash)` signed by the original funding wallet and casino. `startClose(evidence)` is available to the player or casino. `challengeClose(evidence)` accepts strictly newer supported sequences from any submitter before the deadline; re-submitting the proposed sequence reverts. Channel status and the uint64 deadline share a storage slot with the player address; a timestamp that cannot represent the full challenge period is rejected. Challenges never extend the deadline. Cooperative close also works during a pending unilateral close, before its deadline, provided the state is at least as new as the proposed closing state and agrees at an equal sequence. At the exact deadline, challenges and cooperative close stop and `finalizeClose(channelId)` becomes available.

Finalization establishes a claim without making any external payment. Collection is a separate transaction. It consumes the channel once, turns its open principal reservation into min(deposit,balance), and records winnings=max(balance-deposit,0). `claim` pays protected principal and allocated winnings without new signatures; rejection reverts only this collection. Paid totals are computed as amount minus remaining principal and winnings. `Step` contains the operation, authorization, seed, secret and casino signature; the next checkpoint is derived, never transmitted in the proof. A missing/withheld result does not create a penalty or debt beyond the latest supported completed state.

Deposits and supported balances are below `MAX_BALANCE = 2^128` wei, so the uint256 aggregate `unpaidWinnings` cannot overflow through any number of independently valid claims and block another player's finalization.

## Settlement priority and authority

The deployer is the immutable casino owner and settlement signer. The constructor takes no arguments. Only the owner can call `withdrawHouse(withdrawalId, recipient, amount)`. It rejects zero/reused IDs, zero amounts, the zero address and the casino contract as recipients, and amounts exceeding withdrawal headroom. A successful withdrawal consumes its ID and emits `HouseWithdrawal(withdrawalId, recipient, amount)`. A failed transfer reverts the ID as well. There is no treasury role or signed Withdrawal relay.

Every signature the contract checks is an EOA signature: channel keys, the casino, and the funding account's close authorization. A contract account that funds a channel exits through `startClose`/`finalizeClose`.

The owner is trusted with all house liquidity: it can create jointly signed checkpoints for its own channels and redeem those claims. A second withdrawal key would not stop that. Users must check their wallet and challenge any stale close before the fixed 24-hour deadline. If they miss it, a lower balance signed earlier can become final. An independent watchtower is optional.

Finalized positive winnings enter a FIFO queue. Funding and claims allocate at most eight queue entries per call; `allocateWinnings(limit)` processes up to 64. Allocation reserves ETH before attempting transfers. A rejecting senior recipient retains its allocation while juniors can receive later money. A beneficiary can call `claimTo(channelId, recipient)`; strangers can only trigger payment to its saved destination.

`houseCash = contract balance - protectedPrincipal - reservedWinnings`. Owner withdrawal headroom is `max(0, balance - protectedPrincipal - total unpaid winnings)`; allocated winnings are already part of that debt. A malicious settlement signer can fabricate claims, but cannot use a junior claim to consume cash ahead of already-finalized senior debt. Active private winnings remain unsecured shared-pool promises.
