# Signed channel protocol

The protocol uses one casino owner for signing and house withdrawals, FIFO finalized winnings allocation, EOA signatures throughout, and beneficiary-authorized `claimTo`. Each deployment has its own database and signing history.

The governing [design constraint](../architecture.md#design-constraint) is simplicity. Clearly disclosed trust assumptions, weaker guarantees, and manual user responsibilities are acceptable tradeoffs for less protocol complexity. Stronger guarantees require an explicit product need.

The EIP-712 domain is name `HookedIn`, version `1`, chain ID, and verifying contract. The canonical typed structures and hash functions are in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts) and [contracts/HookedInCasino.sol](../contracts/HookedInCasino.sol). This is a pure prototype: signed structures and contract layouts can change without preserving older deployments or data. There are no compatibility fields or migrations. Use a fresh deployment and data after an incompatible change.

## Opening

Anyone can call `openChannel(signer)` with a positive ETH deposit, without a casino request or signature. The contract derives `channelId = keccak256(abi.encode(msg.sender, signer, msg.value))`, registers the funding account and channel key, and protects the deposit. The key must be nonzero and each funding-account/key/deposit combination is single-use. One channel may be active per funding account; use a fresh channel key for each opening. Copying another player's transaction cannot reserve that player's channel ID. Binding the deposit also prevents a reorg replacement from changing the deposit under an existing ID.

`Opening(channelId, player, signer, deposit)` is unsigned identity data checked against the contract during registration and independent recovery. The genesis checkpoint has sequence 0, balance=deposit and zero preceding/transition hashes. It supports unilateral closure without any casino response, using the ordinary fixed 24-hour challenge window.

After confirmation, the wallet authenticates with the registered channel key and submits this identity to `/api/channels/:id/activate`. Confirmation depth is two on Sepolia and one on Anvil. The service retains only authenticated, confirmed registrations. Debits and credits need no randomness. Opening and activation create nothing else: the casino names a channel's first [round](#rounds) when the wallet asks for it.

## Test coins

A channel holds one asset. `AssetId` is `'eth' | 'test'`, and `ASSETS` in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts) gives ETH and TEST, both with 18 decimals. An ETH channel is opened and settled on-chain. Test coins are the casino's own play money: nothing about a test channel ever reaches the contract.

A test channel's ID is `keccak256(abi.encode(keccak256("HOOKEDIN/TESTCOINS"), player, signer))`, whose first word is a hash rather than an address, so it can never be the ID of a channel the contract knows. Its deposit is 0. It is opened by `POST /api/channels/:id/activate` with `{opening, asset: 'test'}`: there is no chain to read and nothing to confirm, so it is open as soon as the casino knows it. The casino's test bankroll starts at `TEST_BANKROLL`, ten million test coins.

The faucet is a credit counterparty, like the fund: a claim is an ordinary credit whose details name `FAUCET_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/FAUCET")`, signed by the channel's own key. The casino pays exactly `FAUCET_AMOUNT`, 100 coins, to a test channel that holds fewer than `FAUCET_BELOW`, 10, and refuses anything else with code `not-due`. Faucet coins are newly minted, so a claim does not change the test bankroll.

Every wallet has a test channel, funded or not. A wallet that has deposited nothing plays with test coins, and its channel is filled from the faucet the moment it starts, so a guest can play at once. An account with an open ETH channel plays with its ETH unless the player chooses test coins.

The casino keeps every figure per asset: bankroll, equity, escrow, commissions, developer totals, active liabilities and channel count. `GET /api/status` and `GET /api/metrics` carry a `test` object beside the ETH figures. The [bankroll fund](#the-bankroll-fund) takes ETH only, and a test channel has nothing on-chain to close; both refuse with code `wrong-asset`. Test channels are invisible to chain observation and to a watchtower.

## Checkpoints and transitions

`Checkpoint(channelId, sequence, previousStateHash, transitionHash, balance)` is signed by the channel key and casino. It carries money and position only: randomness belongs to the [round](#rounds) each bet names.

`Operation(channelId, previousStateHash, sequence, kind, amount, prizes, round, seedHash, memo)` binds exact terms and position, where `prizes` is a list of `Prize(rangeStart, rangeEnd, payout)`. An executed operation advances exactly once from the preceding state. Rejection uses a jointly signed checkpoint above the attempted operation, as described below. The contract settles money and reads nothing else: every field a kind does not use must be zero, so each operation has one meaning and one encoding. A bet names its round, its seed hash and 1 to 64 prizes; a debit and a credit leave all three empty; a checkpoint-only proof carries the canonical empty step. Amounts, deposits and balances are below 2^128 wei.

| Kind     | Authorization | Effect                                                                                   |
| -------- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 bet    | Channel key   | balance - amount + every prize holding the outcome; settled by the secret of its `round` |
| 2 debit  | Channel key   | balance - amount                                                                         |
| 3 credit | Channel key   | balance + amount                                                                         |

`memo` is what the operation means to the wallet and the casino: the hash (`hashJSON`, keccak256 of canonical JSON) of its **details**, `{id, game?, group?, counterparty?, bet?}`. `id` is the hash of the wallet's own name for the operation, so an exact retry is the same operation, and an ID cannot be reused with another digest; a game's operation takes its name from the game's own ID, the asset and the game's key, so it is the same on every channel of its player (see [fast exchange](#fast-exchange)). `game` is `{key, developer}`: the [key](#games) of the game that asked for a bet or a payment, and the developer it pays, who earns half of a bet's commission. `group` is a label of up to 64 characters the game gives its bets and payments, such as one hand or one match, so they are shown and found together. `counterparty` is what a debit pays into or a credit collects from: the [bankroll fund](#the-bankroll-fund), a [developer's bank](#a-developers-bank), a [bet that settled later](#bets-that-settle-later), the [developer earnings](#developer-earnings) or the [faucet](#test-coins). `bet` says how a [bet that settles later](#bets-that-settle-later) settles. A bet names its game; a debit names its game (a payment, or a bet that settles later), or what it pays into (an investment, a bank deposit); a credit names what it collects from; only what names a game carries a group. Every field is in one form (a checksummed address, lowercase hashes, decimal strings), so one meaning has one memo. A request carries the details whole beside the signed operation, the casino refuses details that do not hash to the memo, and both sides keep them with the evidence, where `verify-evidence` checks them against it. Commission is not part of the signed operation.

The wallet signs the exact stake and every prize. The service calculates the even commission, [the edge the bankroll does not need](../architecture.md#settled-trade-offs), using the [round's Kelly condition](economics.md#a-round-is-one-wager) against current unreserved accounting bankroll, and reserves the round's worst-case cash decrease plus commission synchronously before reading a round's secret or awaiting signing. Reservations cover all concurrent channels in the single service process: a channel's own round until its bet commits or fails, a referee's round until it is drawn or its bets are refunded. The service makes its capacity decision before it reads the secret, so no rejection can depend on the outcome, and a bet it has admitted is settled whatever happens to capacity afterwards. A bet on the channel's own round is admitted and priced as it is placed; a bet on a referee's round is admitted as it is placed and priced with the rest of its round when the round is drawn. If capacity is insufficient, it returns a signed rejection checkpoint. Commission is reported in the response to a bet on the channel's own round and retained in the operator ledger; it is not an extra player debit or a wallet-verified fee commitment. The contract reserves no potential winnings. This is per-operation honest-operator risk admission, not collateral or a whole-hand reservation.

A bet's `round` is the hash of a secret and its `seedHash` the hash of a seed, and only that secret and that seed settle it. Outcome is the low 64 bits of `keccak256(abi.encode(keccak256("HOOKEDIN/OUTCOME"), seed, secret))`. **The stake is paid to enter, and every prize whose range holds the outcome pays**: `balance − amount + Σ payout` over the prizes with `rangeStart <= outcome < rangeEnd`. A prize is any non-empty range within `[0, 2^64)` with a positive payout below 2^128. Prizes may overlap, in which case they add; they need no order; an outcome in no prize pays nothing. One prize below a threshold is a coin-flip style wager, a prize per bucket is a Plinko board, a prize smaller than the stake is a partial loss, and a roulette player's chips are simply that player's prizes, overlapping where the chips do. The contract asks nothing more of a bet: whether it is worth taking is the casino's [admission](economics.md) decision, so covering the whole outcome space is legal and a bet that cannot lose is merely one the casino declines. The outcome depends only on the round and its seed, never on the channel or the rest of the operation, so every bet on one round and seed sees the same 64-bit value. Transition hash is `keccak256(abi.encode(hashOperation,secret))`.

An on-chain Step contains only the operation/authorization, the revealed seed and secret, and the casino signature over the derived checkpoint; the contract checks both against the hashes the bet signed. Evidence is either a joint checkpoint (or registered genesis), or that checkpoint plus one Step. The latter is independently usable even if the final player acknowledgment was lost. Games and their local allocations are outside the settlement protocol.

## Rounds

Every bet is on a **round**. The casino draws a secret, keeps it, and names the round by its hash: `round = keccak256(secret)`. A bet signs that name and the hash of a **seed**, `seedHash = keccak256(seed)`, and the round's outcome is a function of the secret and the seed together. Only its one secret and that one seed can settle a bet that signs them, so a round needs no signature of its own; the contract checks exactly those two hashes and knows nothing else about rounds. The casino names a channel's round before that wallet draws its seed, so neither side can know the outcome before both are out. A round is used once: it is **revealed** when it settles, a channel's own also when its bet is declined, and a revealed round settles nothing more.

`POST /api/channels/:id/round` names the round that channel's next bet settles on, `{id}`, and names the same one until it is used. The wallet draws its seed only after it has that name, signs the seed's hash into the bet and sends the bet with the seed, `{request, details, signature, acknowledgment, seed}`, to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations`. The round opens and closes with the bet: the reply is the result, and carries `nextRound`, the round after it, so an ordinary bet stays one request. A bet on a round that is not the channel's next one (a restored backup, a casino that lost its data) is declined like any other, and the reply names the channel's next round.

**A channel's round that settles nobody is revealed all the same.** When the casino declines a bet on it, the rejection carries the round's `secret`: the wallet checks it against the round the bet signed and, with the seed it drew itself, records at once what the bet would have paid. It countersigns nothing less. The one exception is a round the casino says it has lost: that rejection says `lost: true` instead, and the receipt says in words that the bet cannot be checked. A casino that declines wagers by outcome is therefore visible to its players on the receipts themselves, without any protocol penalty.

**Durability.** A round's secret is private service state and is not in the signing history until the round is revealed; the reveal is. A stale database restore replays the reveal, so no secret that was ever public can settle another bet, and a round whose secret was lost is simply unknown.

## Bets that settle later

A bet on the channel's own round settles in the request that places it. A bet that **settles later** is placed the same way, and its stake leaves the balance at once, but its game's **referee** settles it afterwards. It is a debit whose details name the game, any group, and in `bet` the referee, a `deadline`, and either the `round` it rides, the `seedHash` committed to that round and `prizes`, with the round's deadline, or `terms`, with a deadline within 30 days. The reply is the signed result at once: **the bet is final**, and the casino [holds it](../README.md#trust-model): its stake is its player's until the bet settles, and what the bet pays or refunds is the casino's promise until it is collected. Whatever it pays is owed to its player, listed by `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` under the bet's hash and collected with a credit that names it. The bet is known by that hash, the hash of the operation that placed it, which signs its terms. A bet nobody settles by its deadline is refunded.

A game's **referee** is any key the game's publisher names with it. It needs no channel and holds no money of its own, but a split it signs is paid from its developer's bank: keep its key as safe as that bank. It proves itself with `RefereeAccess(address referee, uint256 expiresAt)` signed by that key, sent like a channel's `Access`; the casino turns away a key that referees no published game before it counts against the referees' rate budget. The casino takes a bet that settles later only for the referee its game's publisher named.

| The bet has | Its referee settles it with                             | It pays                                           | Who pays                                                    |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `prizes`    | a draw of its round: the seed committed to it, revealed | what its prizes pay on the round's 64-bit outcome | the bankroll, as for any bet, less commission               |
| `terms`     | a split: `Settlement(bet, player, casino)`              | `player`, and gives the casino `casino`           | the developer's bank, what the two come to beyond the stake |

**A draw** is how players share an outcome against the bankroll: every bet on a referee's round rides one outcome, so the round is one wager. A round is for one game, by its key, in one asset, and the referee opens it before anybody bets on it. `POST /api/rounds` with `{game, asset}`, `game` being the game's key, answers with the game's round that waits to be drawn, or names a new one: the hash of a secret the casino keeps. Only the referee the game's publisher named is given one. A round takes bets for ten minutes from when the casino names it: that is its `deadline`, which every bet on it signs, and its bets are refunded if it is not drawn by then. Then the referee commits the seed it will draw the round with, `POST /api/rounds/:round/commit` with `{seedHash, signature}`, the signature being its `Commit(bytes32 round, bytes32 seedHash)`; a round takes one commitment. Both answer with the round as `GET /api/rounds/:round` shows it to anyone, `{id, game, referee, asset, deadline, status, seedHash?, signature?, seed?, secret?, outcome?}`: `status` is `open`, `drawn`, or `expired` once its deadline has passed undrawn, and a drawn round shows its seed, its secret and their 64-bit outcome. A game's page learns the round from its own server. Before it signs a bet on the round, the wallet reads it, checks that it is open, the game's and in its asset, and that the referee signed its commitment, and names the round, the seed hash and the deadline in the bet: **the bet's outcome is fixed before it is placed**, and neither the casino nor the referee knows it.

The casino admits each bet as it is placed, **with every bet the round took before it** (see [economics](economics.md#a-round-is-one-wager)), before anybody could know the outcome: a bet that hedges the round is welcome where one that stacks it is not, and one that does not fit is declined with the ordinary signed rejection, "Casino capacity is too low for this bet on this round". The round holds back its worst case until it is drawn or its bets are refunded. A round takes up to 256 bets, all in its asset; a bet on a round that is not open, or is full, is declined. The referee draws its round when it chooses, before its deadline, with `POST /api/rounds/:round/draw` and `{seed}`: one signing-history record reveals the round and owes every bet it took what its prizes pay on the outcome of the seed and the secret. Commission is priced over the round then, against the capital there is then; a bankroll that has fallen below what prices the round still pays every bet on it, and takes no commission from it. The reply is `{round, seed?, secret?, outcome?, bets}`; asking again is the same answer, and a round with no open bets is not drawn. The referee chooses when a round is drawn, and it can hold a bet no longer than its round's deadline; it cannot choose what any bet pays.

**A split** is how a game settles what no prize table can say: a cash-out at the moment a player chooses it, a match that ends in a month. An automatic cash-out set before the round is a prize, and is drawn, in a crash game with no manual cash-out. A manual cash-out while the curve rises cannot come from a draw: to know when to crash, the referee would have to draw at take-off, and a draw is public, so every page would know the crash point. A crash game with a manual cash-out settles its bets by split, with a crash point its referee keeps to itself: its word. The bet's `terms` are the game's own JSON object, up to 4,096 bytes of canonical JSON, whose numbers are whole, such as a pick and its odds. The referee settles it whenever it chooses with `Settlement(bytes32 bet, uint256 player, uint256 casino)`, signed by its key and posted with others in `POST /api/bets/settle` as `{settlements: [{bet, player, casino, signature}]}`: `player` is what the player is paid and `casino` what the casino is given. The developer's bank keeps the rest of the stake, or pays what the two come to beyond it, over the whole batch at once: a batch the bank cannot pay is refused with code `bank-short`, and nothing in it is settled. A bet already settled answers with what settled it; one past its deadline is refused with `bet-expired`, and refunded. The casino's share is its commission on a split, and the referee's to choose: the casino's policy asks for about half of what the bet is expected to earn the developer, and nothing enforces it. A bet with prizes is only ever drawn, and a bet with terms only ever split.

`GET /api/bets?game=<key>&group=<group>` lists a game's open bets, of one group if it names one, up to 1,000, in the order they were placed, each as `GET /api/bets/:hash` shows a bet to anyone: `{bet, game, group?, asset, uname, alias, referee, stake, placedAt, deadline, status}` with its `round`, `seedHash` and `prizes` or its `terms`, and once settled its `payout`, the `draw` (the seed and the secret) or the `settlement`, and `refunded` if its stake came back. `placedAt` is when the casino took it, so a referee can tell a bet that came too late for its game.

Whatever keeps the casino from taking a signed bet (another referee, a deadline out of range, prizes it cannot read, a round that is not open or is full, a bet the bankroll cannot take with the round's others, a player with 64 bets open in one asset, an operation its player already carried out on another channel) is answered with the ordinary signed rejection, never an error the wallet would get again.

**Account discovery.** Authenticated `GET /api/channels/:id/bets?status=open|settled&after=…&limit=50` returns `{bets, cursor, more}` for the channel's funding account and asset, across its channels. Each is `{bet, game, group?, asset, status, stake, deadline, collected}`, and a settled one adds `payout`, `settledAt` and any `refunded`. `collected` means a positive payout has been credited; a zero payout needs no collection. The default limit is 50, maximum 100. Pass `cursor` as `after` while `more` is true. Open bets are ordered by hash: refresh from the beginning each time, since this is a changing set. Settled bets are ordered by the order they settled in, durable in the signing history: save the cursor and resume from it, including after an empty page. The feed includes zero payouts and refunds.

**Collect.** The wallet works out what a settled bet paid before it signs the credit: for a drawn bet, it checks the seed against the seed hash and the secret against the round, both of which the bet signed, and reads its own prizes against their outcome; for a split, it checks the referee's signature over the bet's hash and the split; a refunded bet pays back its stake. It signs for that amount and nothing else. `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` lists at most 256 payouts at a time; the wallet collects them, and the next reply lists the rest. A player who is away collects whenever their wallet next looks.

**Durability.** Bets, draws and settlements are in the signing history, so a restart or a stale restore loses none of them: an open bet stays open, and one past its deadline is refunded. The worst case a round holds back is kept in memory, and the casino rebuilds it from the open bets when it starts, before it admits anything else. A referee's round is private service state like a channel's own; one whose secret was lost is unknown, its bets are refunded at their deadlines, and the referee is named another.

Trust differs by how a bet settles, and its receipt says so. A draw's outcome is fixed before any bet on it is placed: the casino fixed the round's secret and the referee committed its seed first, and every bet names both, so **nobody can change the outcome**, and neither knows it alone before the draw. Referee and casino together could know it in advance, and decline winning bets as they are placed or leave the round undrawn: a declined bet can be checked against the round's draw, which is public, and a round left undrawn shows in the game's public record as bets its referee let run past their deadline. A split is the referee's word, which its signature makes attributable and nothing more; a game that wants its offer attributable too can put the referee's signed offer in the terms. A referee can stall a bet, never keep it: an unsettled bet is refunded at its deadline.

## A developer's bank

A bet with terms is paid from its game developer's **bank**: a balance at the casino, per asset, that keeps what the referee's splits do not pay and pays what they owe beyond their stakes. Nothing in it is reserved, so a developer can offer odds on events far off without locking up what a win would cost; a settlement the bank cannot pay in full is refused, and the bet is refunded at its deadline if it is never settled. Whether a developer can pay is [outside HookedIn](../architecture.md#settled-trade-offs): playing their game trusts them.

```text
BankStatement(address developer, string asset, uint256 sequence, uint256 balance, bytes32 cause)
Withdraw(address developer, string asset, uint256 amount, uint256 sequence)
```

`BANK_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/BANK")` is its counterparty. **Depositing** is a debit that names it, from the developer's own channel, and the response carries the casino's `BankStatement` with the deposit's hash as `cause`. **Withdrawing** starts with the developer's `Withdraw`, signed by their channel's key and posted to `POST /api/channels/:id/bank/withdraw`; `sequence` is the statement it will produce, so it works once, and asking again returns the recorded statement. The amount is owed to the developer at once, listed under `BANK_ID` and collected with a credit. `GET /api/channels/:id/bank` is the bank as it is now: every split of the developer's bets moves its balance, so the wallet reads it before it withdraws and checks each statement's signature and cause, not its arithmetic.

## Games

A game's **key** is `keccak256(abi.encode(publisher, name))`: made from its publisher and the name they publish it under, and kept whoever it pays and wherever it is served. A bet and a payment sign `game = {key, developer}` in their details, the game's key and the developer it pays commission to, and the casino keeps every game's bets, commission and public record under its key. Publishing records `{name, url, developer, referee?}` in the publisher's profile, from their own funded channel, with `POST /api/channels/:id/games`: `developer` is the address that earns the game's commission, which need not be the publisher and which the manifest at `url` names too, and `referee` is the key that settles the game's [bets that settle later](#bets-that-settle-later). The profile lists each game as `{name, url, developer, referee?, key}`. A wallet that opens `@alias/name` refuses the game if its manifest names another developer than the one it was published with, so a host that is taken over cannot redirect the game's commission. To pay another address, the publisher changes the manifest's developer and publishes the game again; to move hosts, publishes it at the new URL; either way the game keeps its key, its history and its receipts. A game loaded straight from its manifest has the key of that manifest's developer and URL, a name no published game can have.

## The bankroll fund

Anyone with a channel can **invest in the bankroll** and later divest. An investor trusts the casino completely. A share is the casino's signed promise of a part of the bankroll: no contract holds it, the casino alone states what the bankroll is worth, and the bankroll can lose. What the protocol gives the investor is proof of what they hold, and the casino no way to deny it.

```text
ShareStatement(address holder, uint256 sequence, uint256 shares, uint256 amount, uint256 equity, uint256 totalShares, bytes32 cause)
Redeem(address holder, uint256 shares, uint256 sequence)
Fund(uint256 sequence, uint256 totalShares, uint256 houseShares, uint256 equity, uint256 overdrawn, uint256 at)
```

The fund is a counterparty: `FUND_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/BANKROLL")`. **Investing** is a debit that names it, sent to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations` like a payment. No ETH moves: the signed balance falls, so the accounting bankroll, and with it what the casino can cover, rises by the same amount. **Equity** is that bankroll before the worst cases rounds hold back. The price is fixed before anything is awaited: `amount × totalShares / equity` shares, rounded down, are minted to the channel's **player address**, so a holding outlives any one channel. With the first investment the bankroll the house had already built becomes the house's own shares, one per wei. The response carries the casino's `ShareStatement`: the holder's shares afterwards, the amount, the `equity` and `totalShares` just before that fixed the price, and as `cause` the hash of the holder's signed investment. One signing-history record holds the step and the fund change. An amount too small for a share, or a bankroll with nothing left, is declined with the ordinary signed rejection.

**Divesting** starts with the holder's `Redeem`, signed by the key of an open channel of that player and posted to `POST /api/channels/:id/fund/redeem`. `sequence` is the statement it will produce, so it works once; asking again returns the recorded statement. The shares are burned for `shares × equity / totalShares`, rounded down, which leaves the bankroll at once and is owed to the player: listed by `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` under `FUND_ID` and collected with a credit for exactly that amount. A redemption larger than the unreserved bankroll is refused until the bets counting on that money settle. Minting and burning at the going price leave every other share worth what it was.

A holder's evidence is their highest-numbered statement; the casino's only answer to a smaller holding is a later `Redeem` the holder signed. The wallet checks each statement's signature, that it follows its last one, that its cause is the exact investment or redemption it signed, and that the shares and amount are what the stated price implies. It signs for redeemed money only in an amount one of its statements priced. It cannot check `equity` or `totalShares` themselves: players' balances are private, so the price is the casino's word. `GET /api/fund` returns that word signed and timestamped, a quote the casino can be held to, and `GET /api/channels/:id/fund` the player's holding with its statement.

**The owner's capital.** Everything the chain can show moves pool cash and a liability together (deposits, closures, claims), except the owner adding to the pool or withdrawing from it. At each committed observation any such change in equity is the house's: it is issued, or gives up, shares at the price just before, in a signing-history record of its own, so the price does not move. The house cannot give up shares it does not have. That excess is a loss every holder bears, and is reported as `overdrawn` from then on. While an investor's channel is open the contract still protects their original deposit, so the owner cannot withdraw it; that protection ends with the channel, and the shares remain a promise. Owner capital that moves while the service is down with its database lost is not attributed.

## Developer earnings

Half of every completed bet's commission accrues to the `developer` the bet signed. It is owed to that **address** and collected like a redemption. The casino keeps a running tally per developer address and per asset: what that address has earned, and what of it is collected. Because every bet also signs its `game`, the casino keeps that tally per game as well, and a developer of several games sees which one earned what.

A developer sees the tally in their own channel. Whenever that address has ever earned anything, `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` carries `{source: DEVELOPER_ID, index: 0, amount, earned, collected}`, where `DEVELOPER_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/DEVELOPER")` and `amount` is `earned − collected` and may be "0".

It is collected with an ordinary channel opened from that address: a credit whose counterparty is `DEVELOPER_ID`, signed by that channel's key. There is nothing to check, so the wallet signs it by itself. The casino accepts a credit for no more than is due and refuses anything else with code `not-due`. Nothing moves on-chain and the bankroll does not change: money the casino already owed the developer becomes that developer's signed balance, which settles like any other.

The tally is the casino's word: it cannot be held to having counted every bet. Bets are private to their channels; a player's receipt shows the commission of each bet on the channel's own round, and the [admission rule](economics.md) that fixes it is public.

## Fast exchange

1. Wallet saves the exact request and channel-key signature. The request carries no checkpoint signature: the casino never holds a player-signed checkpoint above a wager it has not yet decided.
2. Casino requires acknowledgment of its previous response, validates the authorization and exact terms, and serializes the channel position.
3. Casino commits either the signed result or its signature over an unchanged-balance rejection checkpoint at n+2 to its signing history and result store before sending it. Commission accrues only on an executed bet. A declined bet's round, when it is the channel's own, is revealed in the same record.
4. Wallet verifies the result or rejection, countersigns the new checkpoint, and saves the evidence, receipt and cleared pending operation together before returning to the game. The countersignature travels as the acknowledgment of the next request.

The rejection checkpoint retains the base balance. Its `previousStateHash` identifies the base and its `transitionHash` is the exact rejected operation digest. Nothing is settled on the declined round. Once the wallet countersigns it, the existing contract supports it without a new transition kind; until then the jointly signed base settles to the identical balance, so the casino defends with the base and loses nothing. After saving rejection n+2, the wallet can submit a new bet at n+3. The higher checkpoint can challenge an attempted settlement of the rejected n+1 wager; the player must still meet the existing challenge deadline. Because the player signs the rejection only after receiving it, a completed result at n+1 can never be superseded by a rejection the casino signs later.

The next request can carry acknowledgment. Retrying an operation ID returns the same accepted or rejected receipt; a new attempt needs a new ID. A game's operation belongs to its player, not to a channel: one its player already carried out on another channel is declined with a signed rejection that carries `used: true` and the reason "This operation was carried out on another channel", and a bet on the channel's own round reveals the round as any decline does. The wallet tells the game `id-used`. Only a verified joint rejection permits the wallet to clear a rejected signed wager. An HTTP error or timeout leaves it unresolved: recover the exact request or close. Previously committed results remain recoverable during stale chain observations, even when new admission is paused. The wallet has its round before it draws each bet's seed.

Unsigned bankroll information in responses and `/api/metrics` is a planning hint, not an admission promise. The wallet sends the selected wager directly; the service accepts it or returns a signed rejection. Deploy the contract, wallet and service from the same prototype revision: `GET /api/config` carries `protocol` (`PROTOCOL` in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts)), the hash of every typed structure and of the rules the two sides apply alike: the operation kinds, the outcome tag, the counterparty IDs and every limit. `limits` in the same reply are those bounds, `{prizes, outcomeSpace, bets, cells, round, deadline, terms, group}`, the numbers the wallet reports to a game. `refereeProtocol` hashes only what a referee shares with the casino (`RefereeAccess`, `Commit`, `Settlement`, the outcome and the limits), so a change to what only wallets sign does not stop a referee. A wallet or a referee built against another revision stops there, before it signs anything. Every refusal of the API is `{error, code}`: the text is for people and the `code` is what a client acts on, such as `wrong-seed`, `bank-short`, `unacknowledged`, `paused`, `rate-limited` or `not-found`.

## Closing

`cooperativeClose(evidence, playerCloseSignature, casinoCloseSignature)` uses `Close(channelId,stateHash)` signed by the original funding wallet and casino. `startClose(evidence)` is available to the player or casino. `challengeClose(evidence)` accepts strictly newer supported sequences from any submitter before the deadline; re-submitting the proposed sequence reverts. Channel status and the uint64 deadline share a storage slot with the player address; a timestamp that cannot represent the full challenge period is rejected. Challenges never extend the deadline. Cooperative close also works during a pending unilateral close, before its deadline, provided the state is at least as new as the proposed closing state and agrees at an equal sequence. At the exact deadline, challenges and cooperative close stop and `finalizeClose(channelId)` becomes available.

Finalization establishes a claim without making any external payment. Collection is a separate transaction. It consumes the channel once, turns its open principal reservation into min(deposit,balance), and records winnings=max(balance-deposit,0). `claim` pays protected principal and allocated winnings without new signatures; rejection reverts only this collection. Paid totals are computed as amount minus remaining principal and winnings. `Step` contains the operation, authorization, seed, secret and casino signature; the next checkpoint is derived, never transmitted in the proof. A missing/withheld result does not create a penalty or debt beyond the latest supported completed state.

Deposits and supported balances are below `MAX_BALANCE = 2^128` wei, so the uint256 aggregate `unpaidWinnings` cannot overflow through any number of independently valid claims and block another player's finalization.

## Settlement priority and authority

The deployer is the immutable casino owner and settlement signer. The constructor takes no arguments. Only the owner can call `withdrawHouse(withdrawalId, recipient, amount)`. It rejects zero/reused IDs, zero amounts, the zero address and the casino contract as recipients, and amounts exceeding withdrawal headroom. A successful withdrawal consumes its ID and emits `HouseWithdrawal(withdrawalId, recipient, amount)`. A failed transfer reverts the ID as well. There is no treasury role or signed Withdrawal relay.

Every signature the contract checks is an EOA signature: channel keys, the casino, and the funding account's close authorization. A contract account that funds a channel exits through `startClose`/`finalizeClose`.

The owner is trusted with all house liquidity: it can create jointly signed checkpoints for its own channels and redeem those claims. A second withdrawal key would not stop that. Users must check their wallet and challenge any stale close before the fixed 24-hour deadline. If they miss it, a lower balance signed earlier can become final. An independent watchtower is optional.

Finalized positive winnings enter a FIFO queue. Funding and claims allocate at most eight queue entries per call; `allocateWinnings(limit)` processes up to 64. Allocation reserves ETH before attempting transfers. A rejecting senior recipient retains its allocation while juniors can receive later money. A beneficiary can call `claimTo(channelId, recipient)`; strangers can only trigger payment to its saved destination.

`houseCash = contract balance - protectedPrincipal - reservedWinnings`. Owner withdrawal headroom is `max(0, balance - protectedPrincipal - total unpaid winnings)`; allocated winnings are already part of that debt. A malicious settlement signer can fabricate claims, but cannot use a junior claim to consume cash ahead of already-finalized senior debt. Active private winnings remain unsecured shared-pool promises.
