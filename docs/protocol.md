# Signed channel protocol

The protocol uses one casino owner for signing and house withdrawals, FIFO finalized winnings allocation, EOA signatures throughout, and beneficiary-authorized `claimTo`. Each deployment has its own database and signing history.

The governing [design constraint](../architecture.md#design-constraint) is simplicity. Clearly disclosed trust assumptions, weaker guarantees, and manual user responsibilities are acceptable tradeoffs for less protocol complexity. Stronger guarantees require an explicit product need.

The EIP-712 domain is name `HookedIn`, version `1`, chain ID, and verifying contract. The canonical typed structures and hash functions are in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts) and [contracts/HookedInCasino.sol](../contracts/HookedInCasino.sol). This is a pure prototype: signed structures and contract layouts can change without preserving older deployments or data. There are no compatibility fields or migrations. Use a fresh deployment and data after an incompatible change.

## Opening

Anyone can call `openChannel(signer)` with a positive ETH deposit, without a casino request or signature. The contract derives `channelId = keccak256(abi.encode(msg.sender, signer, msg.value))`, registers the funding account and channel key, and protects the deposit. The key must be nonzero and each funding-account/key/deposit combination is single-use. One channel may be active per funding account; use a fresh channel key for each opening. Copying another player's transaction cannot reserve that player's channel ID. Binding the deposit also prevents a reorg replacement from changing the deposit under an existing ID.

`Opening(channelId, player, signer, deposit)` is unsigned identity data checked against the contract during registration and independent recovery. The genesis checkpoint has sequence 0, balance=deposit and zero preceding/transition hashes. It supports unilateral closure without any casino response, using the ordinary fixed 24-hour challenge window.

After confirmation, the wallet authenticates with the registered channel key and submits this identity to `/api/channels/:id/activate`. Confirmation depth is two on Sepolia and one on Anvil. The service retains only authenticated, confirmed registrations. Payments and transfers need no randomness. Opening and activation create nothing else: the casino names a channel's first [round](#rounds) when the wallet asks for it.

## Test coins

A channel holds one asset. `AssetId` is `'eth' | 'test'`, and `ASSETS` in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts) gives ETH and TEST, both with 18 decimals. An ETH channel is opened and settled on-chain. Test coins are the casino's own play money: nothing about a test channel ever reaches the contract.

A test channel's ID is `keccak256(abi.encode(keccak256("HOOKEDIN/TESTCOINS"), player, signer))`, whose first word is a hash rather than an address, so it can never be the ID of a channel the contract knows. Its deposit is 0. It is opened by `POST /api/channels/:id/activate` with `{opening, asset: 'test'}`: there is no chain to read and nothing to confirm, so it is open as soon as the casino knows it. The casino's test bankroll starts at `TEST_BANKROLL`, ten million test coins.

The faucet is a credit counterparty, like the fund: a claim is an ordinary kind-4 credit naming `FAUCET_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/FAUCET")`, signed by the channel's own key. The casino pays exactly `FAUCET_AMOUNT`, 100 coins, to a test channel that holds fewer than `FAUCET_BELOW`, 10, and refuses anything else with code `not-due`. Faucet coins are newly minted, so a claim does not change the test bankroll.

Every wallet has a test channel, funded or not. A wallet that has deposited nothing plays with test coins, and its channel is filled from the faucet the moment it starts, so a guest can play at once. An account with an open ETH channel plays with its ETH unless the player chooses test coins.

The casino keeps every figure per asset: bankroll, equity, escrow, commissions, developer totals, active liabilities and channel count. `GET /api/status` and `GET /api/metrics` carry a `test` object beside the ETH figures. The [bankroll fund](#the-bankroll-fund) takes ETH only, and a test channel has nothing on-chain to close; both refuse with code `wrong-asset`. Test channels are invisible to chain observation and to a watchtower.

## Checkpoints and transitions

`Checkpoint(channelId, sequence, previousStateHash, transitionHash, balance)` is signed by the channel key and casino. It carries money and position only: randomness belongs to the [round](#rounds) each bet names.

`Operation(channelId, previousStateHash, sequence, kind, amount, prizes, seedHash, round, operationId, game, developer, counterparty)` binds exact terms and position, where `prizes` is a list of `Prize(rangeStart, rangeEnd, payout)`. An executed operation advances exactly once from the preceding state. Rejection uses a jointly signed checkpoint above the attempted operation, as described below. The operation ID cannot be reused with another digest. Every field a kind does not use must be zero, so each operation has one meaning and one encoding: a bet names a non-zero `developer` and 1 to 64 prizes, every other kind leaves the developer zero and the prizes empty, and a checkpoint-only proof carries the canonical empty step. `game` is the game that asked for the operation, the hash of the manifest URL it was published under: a bet and a payment are what a game debits with no counterparty of its own, so both name it, and an investment and a payout leave it zero. Amounts, deposits and balances are below 2^128 wei. Commission is not part of the signed operation.

| Kind       | Authorization | Effect                                                                                                                                |
| ---------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 bet      | Channel key   | balance - amount + every prize holding the outcome; settled by the secret of its `round`                                              |
| 2 payment  | Channel key   | balance - amount; no round, commission, or ETH transfer                                                                               |
| 3 transfer | Channel key   | balance - amount; counterparty is the [bankroll fund](#the-bankroll-fund): an investment                                              |
| 4 receive  | Channel key   | balance + amount; counterparty is what pays out: the fund, the [developer earnings](#developer-earnings) or the [faucet](#test-coins) |

The wallet signs the exact stake and every prize. The service calculates the even commission using the [round's Kelly condition](economics.md#a-round-is-one-wager) against current unreserved accounting bankroll and reserves the round's worst-case cash decrease plus commission synchronously before reading a round's secret or awaiting signing. Reservations cover all concurrent channels in the single service process until commit or failure. The service makes its capacity decision before it reads the secret, so no rejection can depend on the outcome, and a bet it has admitted is settled whatever happens to capacity afterwards: only its commission gives way. If capacity is insufficient, it returns a signed rejection checkpoint. Commission is reported in the response and retained in the operator ledger; it is not an extra player debit or a wallet-verified fee commitment. The contract reserves no potential winnings. This is per-operation honest-operator risk admission, not collateral or a whole-hand reservation.

A bet's `round` is the hash of a secret and its `seedHash` the hash of a seed, and only that secret and that seed settle it. Outcome is the low 64 bits of `keccak256(abi.encode(keccak256("HOOKEDIN/OUTCOME"), seed, secret))`. **The stake is paid to enter, and every prize whose range holds the outcome pays**: `balance − amount + Σ payout` over the prizes with `rangeStart <= outcome < rangeEnd`. A prize is any non-empty range within `[0, 2^64)` with a positive payout below 2^128. Prizes may overlap, in which case they add; they need no order; an outcome in no prize pays nothing. One prize below a threshold is a coin-flip style wager, a prize per bucket is a Plinko board, a prize smaller than the stake is a partial loss, and a roulette player's chips are simply that player's prizes, overlapping where the chips do. The contract asks nothing more of a bet: whether it is worth taking is the casino's [admission](economics.md) decision, so covering the whole outcome space is legal and a bet that cannot lose is merely one the casino declines. The outcome depends only on the round and its seed, never on the channel or the rest of the operation, so every bet on one round and seed sees the same 64-bit value. Transition hash is `keccak256(abi.encode(hashOperation,secret))`.

An on-chain Step contains only the operation/authorization, the revealed seed and secret, and the casino signature over the derived checkpoint; the contract checks both against the hashes the bet signed. Evidence is either a joint checkpoint (or registered genesis), or that checkpoint plus one Step. The latter is independently usable even if the final player acknowledgment was lost. Games and their local allocations are outside the settlement protocol.

## Rounds

Every bet is on a **round**. The casino draws a secret, keeps it, and names the round by its hash: `round = keccak256(secret)`. A bet signs that name and the hash of a **seed**, `seedHash = keccak256(seed)`, and the round's outcome is a function of the secret and the seed together. Only its one secret and that one seed can settle a bet that signs them, so a round needs no signature of its own; the contract checks exactly those two hashes and knows nothing else about rounds. Each side holds only the hash of what the other drew, so neither can know the outcome before both are out: the casino names a channel's own round before that wallet draws its seed, and a host sends only the hash of its seed before the casino draws the secret. A round is used once: it is **revealed** when it settles its bets, and also when it is declined or abandoned, and a revealed round settles nothing more. A round is bet on in one asset: a channel's own round in the channel's asset, a hosted round in the one its host asked for. A bet in the other asset is refused with code `wrong-asset`.

```text
open(seedHash)  ────────close(seed)────────▶  revealed     a hosted round
created  ──────────── a channel's own bet ─────────▶  revealed
```

**A channel's own round.** `POST /api/channels/:id/round` names the round that channel's next bet settles on, `{id}`, and names the same one until it is used. The wallet draws its seed only after it has that name, signs the seed's hash into the bet and sends the bet with the seed, `{request, signature, acknowledgment, seed}`, to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations`. The round opens and closes with the bet: the reply is the result, and carries `nextRound`, the round after it, so an ordinary bet stays one request. Neither side could choose the outcome: the casino committed first and the player's seed came second. A bet on a round that is not the channel's next one (a restored backup, a casino that lost its data) is declined like any other, and the reply names the channel's next round. The rejection carries the round's secret when that round is revealed; otherwise the casino has no secret this channel may see, and the rejection says the round is `lost` instead.

**A hosted round** lets many channels share one outcome. Its **host** is any key: it needs no channel and holds no money, and proves itself with `HostAccess(address host, uint256 expiresAt)` signed by that key, sent like a channel's `Access`.

1. **Open.** `POST /api/rounds` with `{seedHash, asset, window}` returns the round, open at once; without an asset the round is ETH. Only the host names the seed, so no seat can, and the casino sees only its hash, so it can draw the round's secret afterwards without knowing what it would settle. The host keeps the seed itself until the close, so the casino cannot know the outcome while it admits seats. `window` is the betting time the game needs, 1 second to a minute, and it runs from the round's **first seat**: what holds a player's money is the time between their bet and the close, so the host asks for the time its game takes and no more. A round nobody has joined holds nothing and waits ten minutes for its first seat.
2. **Join.** Each player's own wallet sends its bet, naming that round and that seed hash, to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations`, exactly as it sends any bet. The reply is `{status: 'seated', operationId}`: nothing is signed yet, and sending the same request again is how the wallet later finds the result. Ranges express the game: equal ranges pay together, disjoint ranges never both pay, nested ranges pay in order. Seats are admitted **one at a time against the whole round** (see [economics](economics.md#a-round-is-one-wager)): a seat that does not fit gets its own signed rejection and costs no other seat anything, and a seat that hedges the round is welcome where one that stacks it was not. A malformed bet (bad signature, another seed hash, insufficient balance, a channel that is closed or has not acknowledged its previous response) fails its own request with nothing reserved or signed. A seat holds its channel's next position, so nothing else can be signed there meanwhile. A round holds at most 256 seats, one per channel, and at most 128 distinct total payouts across its outcome space.
3. **Close.** `POST /api/rounds/:id/close` with `{seed}`, the seed whose hash the round was opened with; any other is refused with code `wrong-seed` and reveals nothing. The casino reveals the secret and settles every seat on the one outcome, in one signing-history record `{round: {id, host, asset, secret, seed}, responses}` written before anything leaves the writer. Commission is decided once, over the seats the round closed with. The reply is the revealed round, so a shared round runs on two requests. Closing again after a lost reply is the same answer.

`GET /api/rounds/:id` is public by ID, which the players of a round know: `{id, host, asset, status, seedHash, seed, expiresAt, seats, secret, bankroll}`, with each seat `{uname, alias, stake, prizes}` while the round is open, and the `seed` and `secret` once its host has closed it.

**Leaving.** A player gives up a seat by presenting the signed bet to `POST /api/channels/:id/cancel` as `{request, signature, acknowledgment}`: the casino frees the seat and returns the ordinary signed rejection above the bet, or the recorded result if the host closed the round first. Different chips are a new bet: the player gives up the seat and joins again. The wallet saves a hosted bet as its pending operation and never depends on the host for recovery. The last seat to leave gives the round its ten minutes back, so a table that empties waits for a new first player instead of running out.

**A round that settles nobody is revealed all the same.** When the casino declines a bet on a channel's own round, the rejection carries the round's `secret`: the wallet checks it against the round the bet signed and, with the seed it drew itself, records at once what the bet would have paid. It countersigns nothing less. The one exception is a round the casino says it has lost: that rejection says `lost: true` instead, and the receipt says in words that the bet cannot be checked. A seat of a hosted round that was declined for capacity, or that its player gave up, learns the seed and the secret when its round closes, from `GET /api/rounds/:id`. A hosted round left open past its betting time is revealed by the casino and every seat declined; its seed never came out, so that round had no outcome anybody knew. A casino that declines wagers by outcome is therefore visible to its players on the receipts themselves, without any protocol penalty.

**Durability.** A round's secret is private service state and is not in the signing history until the round is revealed; the reveal is. A stale database restore replays the reveal, so no secret that was ever public can settle another bet, and a round whose secret was lost is simply unknown. A hosted round and its seats are kept in memory: a restart reveals every one of them, so the round settles nobody, its host opens another, and the wallets holding signed bets withdraw them.

Trust in a hosted round differs from a player's own round and wallets say so: only a game whose manifest declares that it runs shared rounds may place a hosted bet, the player allows host-drawn randomness before that game is opened at all, and every hosted receipt is marked. The host cannot choose the outcome alone (it never knows the secret) and the casino cannot alone (it draws the secret knowing only the hash of the seed), but **host and casino together can**. A game developer was already trusted for its rules; a hosted round extends that trust to the randomness of the atomic wager. Neither knows the outcome while seats are taken: the casino sees only the seed's hash until the host closes the round, so it cannot admit or decline a seat by what the seat would win. A host can leave a round open, which costs the players nothing but the round. A public beacon cannot simply stand in for the host's seed: a bet signs the seed's hash before the round closes, so a beacon value would have to be committed to in some other way, and that is a protocol change.

## The bankroll fund

Anyone with a channel can **invest in the bankroll** and later divest. An investor trusts the casino completely. A share is the casino's signed promise of a part of the bankroll: no contract holds it, the casino alone states what the bankroll is worth, and the bankroll can lose. What the protocol gives the investor is proof of what they hold, and the casino no way to deny it.

```text
ShareStatement(address holder, uint256 sequence, uint256 shares, uint256 amount, uint256 equity, uint256 totalShares, bytes32 cause)
Redeem(address holder, uint256 shares, uint256 sequence)
Fund(uint256 sequence, uint256 totalShares, uint256 houseShares, uint256 equity, uint256 overdrawn, uint256 at)
```

The fund is the counterparty of every transfer: `FUND_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/BANKROLL")`. **Investing** is a kind-3 transfer that names it, sent to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations` like a payment. No ETH moves: the signed balance falls, so the accounting bankroll, and with it what the casino can cover, rises by the same amount. **Equity** is that bankroll before in-flight reservations. The price is fixed before anything is awaited: `amount × totalShares / equity` shares, rounded down, are minted to the channel's **player address**, so a holding outlives any one channel. With the first investment the bankroll the house had already built becomes the house's own shares, one per wei. The response carries the casino's `ShareStatement`: the holder's shares afterwards, the amount, the `equity` and `totalShares` just before that fixed the price, and as `cause` the hash of the holder's signed transfer. One signing-history record holds the step and the fund change. An amount too small for a share, or a bankroll with nothing left, is declined with the ordinary signed rejection.

**Divesting** starts with the holder's `Redeem`, signed by the key of an open channel of that player and posted to `POST /api/channels/:id/fund/redeem`. `sequence` is the statement it will produce, so it works once; asking again returns the recorded statement. The shares are burned for `shares × equity / totalShares`, rounded down, which leaves the bankroll at once and is owed to the player: listed by `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` under `FUND_ID` and collected with a kind-4 credit for exactly that amount. A redemption larger than the unreserved bankroll is refused until the bets counting on that money settle. Minting and burning at the going price leave every other share worth what it was.

A holder's evidence is their highest-numbered statement; the casino's only answer to a smaller holding is a later `Redeem` the holder signed. The wallet checks each statement's signature, that it follows its last one, that its cause is the exact transfer or redemption it signed, and that the shares and amount are what the stated price implies. It signs for redeemed money only in an amount one of its statements priced. It cannot check `equity` or `totalShares` themselves: players' balances are private, so the price is the casino's word. `GET /api/fund` returns that word signed and timestamped, a quote the casino can be held to, and `GET /api/channels/:id/fund` the player's holding with its statement.

**The owner's capital.** Everything the chain can show moves pool cash and a liability together (deposits, closures, claims), except the owner adding to the pool or withdrawing from it. At each committed observation any such change in equity is the house's: it is issued, or gives up, shares at the price just before, in a signing-history record of its own, so the price does not move. The house cannot give up shares it does not have. That excess is a loss every holder bears, and is reported as `overdrawn` from then on. While an investor's channel is open the contract still protects their original deposit, so the owner cannot withdraw it; that protection ends with the channel, and the shares remain a promise. Owner capital that moves while the service is down with its database lost is not attributed.

## Developer earnings

Half of every completed bet's commission accrues to the `developer` the bet signed. It is owed to that **address** and collected like a redemption. The casino keeps a running tally per developer address and per asset: what that address has earned, and what of it is collected. Because every bet also signs its `game`, the casino keeps that tally per game as well, and a developer publishing several sees which one earned what.

A developer sees the tally in their own channel. Whenever that address has ever earned anything, `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` carries `{source: DEVELOPER_ID, index: 0, amount, earned, collected}`, where `DEVELOPER_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/DEVELOPER")` and `amount` is `earned − collected` and may be "0".

It is collected with an ordinary channel opened from that address: a kind-4 credit whose `counterparty` is `DEVELOPER_ID`, signed by that channel's key. There is nothing to check, so the wallet signs it by itself. The casino accepts a credit for no more than is due and refuses anything else with code `not-due`. Nothing moves on-chain and the bankroll does not change: money the casino already owed the developer becomes that developer's signed balance, which settles like any other.

The tally is the casino's word: it cannot be held to having counted every bet. Bets are private to their channels; a player's receipt shows the commission of each, and the [admission rule](economics.md) that fixes it is public.

## Fast exchange

1. Wallet saves the exact request and channel-key signature. The request carries no checkpoint signature: the casino never holds a player-signed checkpoint above a wager it has not yet decided.
2. Casino requires acknowledgment of its previous response, validates the authorization and exact terms, and serializes the channel position.
3. Casino commits either the signed result or its signature over an unchanged-balance rejection checkpoint at n+2 to its signing history and result store before sending it. Commission accrues only on an executed bet. A declined bet's round is revealed in the same record.
4. Wallet verifies the result or rejection, countersigns the new checkpoint, and saves the evidence, receipt and cleared pending operation together before returning to the game. The countersignature travels as the acknowledgment of the next request.

The rejection checkpoint retains the base balance. Its `previousStateHash` identifies the base and its `transitionHash` is the exact rejected operation digest. Nothing is settled on the declined round. Once the wallet countersigns it, the existing contract supports it without a new transition kind; until then the jointly signed base settles to the identical balance, so the casino defends with the base and loses nothing. After saving rejection n+2, the wallet can submit a new bet at n+3. The higher checkpoint can challenge an attempted settlement of the rejected n+1 wager; the player must still meet the existing challenge deadline. Because the player signs the rejection only after receiving it, a completed result at n+1 can never be superseded by a rejection the casino signs later.

The next request can carry acknowledgment. Retrying an operation ID returns the same accepted or rejected receipt; a new attempt needs a new ID. Only a verified joint rejection permits the wallet to clear a rejected signed wager. An HTTP error or timeout leaves it unresolved: recover the exact request or close. Previously committed results remain recoverable during stale chain observations, even when new admission is paused. The wallet has its round before it draws each bet's seed.

Unsigned bankroll information in responses and `/api/metrics` is a planning hint, not an admission promise. The wallet sends the selected wager directly; the service accepts it or returns a signed rejection. Deploy the contract, wallet and service from the same prototype revision: `GET /api/config` carries `protocol`, the hash of every signed structure (`PROTOCOL` in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts)), and a wallet or a host built against other structures stops there, before it signs anything.Every refusal of the API is `{error, code}`: the text is for people and the `code` is what a client acts on, such as `round-not-open`, `unacknowledged`, `paused`, `rate-limited` or `not-found`.

## Closing

`cooperativeClose(evidence, playerCloseSignature, casinoCloseSignature)` uses `Close(channelId,stateHash)` signed by the original funding wallet and casino. `startClose(evidence)` is available to the player or casino. `challengeClose(evidence)` accepts strictly newer supported sequences from any submitter before the deadline; re-submitting the proposed sequence reverts. Channel status and the uint64 deadline share a storage slot with the player address; a timestamp that cannot represent the full challenge period is rejected. Challenges never extend the deadline. Cooperative close also works during a pending unilateral close, before its deadline, provided the state is at least as new as the proposed closing state and agrees at an equal sequence. At the exact deadline, challenges and cooperative close stop and `finalizeClose(channelId)` becomes available.

Finalization establishes a claim without making any external payment. Collection is a separate transaction. It consumes the channel once, turns its open principal reservation into min(deposit,balance), and records winnings=max(balance-deposit,0). `claim` pays protected principal and allocated winnings without new signatures; rejection reverts only this collection. Paid totals are computed as amount minus remaining principal and winnings. `Step` contains the operation, authorization, seed, secret and casino signature; the next checkpoint is derived, never transmitted in the proof. A missing/withheld result does not create a penalty or debt beyond the latest supported completed state.

Deposits and supported balances are below `MAX_BALANCE = 2^128` wei, so the uint256 aggregate `unpaidWinnings` cannot overflow through any number of independently valid claims and block another player's finalization.

## Settlement priority and authority

The deployer is the immutable casino owner and settlement signer. The constructor takes no arguments. Only the owner can call `withdrawHouse(withdrawalId, recipient, amount)`. It rejects zero/reused IDs, zero amounts, the zero address and the casino contract as recipients, and amounts exceeding withdrawal headroom. A successful withdrawal consumes its ID and emits `HouseWithdrawal(withdrawalId, recipient, amount)`. A failed transfer reverts the ID as well. There is no treasury role or signed Withdrawal relay.

Every signature the contract checks is an EOA signature: channel keys, the casino, and the funding account's close authorization. A contract account that funds a channel exits through `startClose`/`finalizeClose`.

The owner is trusted with all house liquidity: it can create jointly signed checkpoints for its own channels and redeem those claims. A second withdrawal key would not stop that. Users must check their wallet and challenge any stale close before the fixed 24-hour deadline. If they miss it, a lower balance signed earlier can become final. An independent watchtower is optional.

Finalized positive winnings enter a FIFO queue. Funding and claims allocate at most eight queue entries per call; `allocateWinnings(limit)` processes up to 64. Allocation reserves ETH before attempting transfers. A rejecting senior recipient retains its allocation while juniors can receive later money. A beneficiary can call `claimTo(channelId, recipient)`; strangers can only trigger payment to its saved destination.

`houseCash = contract balance - protectedPrincipal - reservedWinnings`. Owner withdrawal headroom is `max(0, balance - protectedPrincipal - total unpaid winnings)`; allocated winnings are already part of that debt. A malicious settlement signer can fabricate claims, but cannot use a junior claim to consume cash ahead of already-finalized senior debt. Active private winnings remain unsecured shared-pool promises.
