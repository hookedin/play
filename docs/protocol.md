# Signed channel protocol

The protocol uses one casino owner for signing and house withdrawals, FIFO finalized winnings allocation, EOA signatures throughout, and beneficiary-authorized `claimTo`. Each deployment has its own database and signing history.

The governing [design constraint](../architecture.md#design-constraint) is simplicity. Clearly disclosed trust assumptions, weaker guarantees, and manual user responsibilities are acceptable tradeoffs for less protocol complexity. Stronger guarantees require an explicit product need.

The EIP-712 domain is name `HookedIn`, version `1`, chain ID, and verifying contract. The canonical typed structures and hash functions are in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts) and [contracts/HookedInCasino.sol](../contracts/HookedInCasino.sol). This is a pure prototype: signed structures and contract layouts can change without preserving older deployments or data. There are no compatibility fields or migrations. Use a fresh deployment and data after an incompatible change.

## Opening

Anyone can call `openChannel(signer)` with a positive ETH deposit, without a casino request or signature. The contract derives `channelId = keccak256(abi.encode(msg.sender, signer, msg.value))`, registers the funding account and channel key, and protects the deposit. The key must be nonzero and each funding-account/key/deposit combination is single-use. One channel may be active per funding account; use a fresh channel key for each opening. Copying another player's transaction cannot reserve that player's channel ID. Binding the deposit also prevents a reorg replacement from changing the deposit under an existing ID.

`Opening(channelId, player, signer, deposit)` is unsigned identity data checked against the contract during registration and independent recovery. The genesis checkpoint has sequence 0, balance=deposit and zero preceding/transition hashes. It supports unilateral closure without any casino response, using the ordinary fixed 24-hour challenge window.

After confirmation, the wallet authenticates with the registered channel key and submits this identity to `/api/channels/:id/activate`. Confirmation depth is two on Sepolia and one on Anvil. The service retains only authenticated, confirmed registrations. Payments and transfers need no randomness. Opening or activation retries do not generate chains; the casino creates a channel's private chain when its first [round](#rounds) is requested.

## Checkpoints and transitions

`Checkpoint(channelId, sequence, previousStateHash, transitionHash, balance)` is signed by the channel key and casino. It carries money and position only: randomness belongs to the [round](#rounds) each bet names.

`Operation(channelId, previousStateHash, sequence, kind, amount, prizes, seed, roundHead, operationId, developer, counterparty)` binds exact terms and position, where `prizes` is a list of `Prize(rangeStart, rangeEnd, payout)`. An executed operation advances exactly once from the preceding state. Rejection uses a jointly signed checkpoint above the attempted operation, as described below. The operation ID cannot be reused with another digest. Every field a kind does not use must be zero, so each operation has one meaning and one encoding: a bet names a non-zero `developer` and 1 to 64 prizes, every other kind leaves the developer zero and the prizes empty, and a checkpoint-only proof carries the canonical empty step. Amounts, deposits and balances are below 2^128 wei. Commission is not part of the signed operation.

| Kind       | Authorization         | Effect                                                                                                                                |
| ---------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 bet      | Channel key           | balance - amount + every prize holding the outcome; settled by the preimage of its `roundHead`                                        |
| 2 payment  | Channel key           | balance - amount; no preimage, commission, or ETH transfer                                                                            |
| 4 transfer | Sender channel key    | balance - amount; counterparty is the receiving channel, the [table](#tables) bought into, or the [bankroll fund](#the-bankroll-fund) |
| 5 receive  | Recipient channel key | balance + amount; counterparty is the sending channel (operationId is the outgoing operation digest), or the table or fund paying out |

The wallet signs the exact stake and every prize. The service calculates the even commission using the [round's Kelly condition](economics.md#a-round-is-one-wager) against current unreserved accounting bankroll and reserves the round's worst-case cash decrease plus commission synchronously before reading a preimage or awaiting signing. Reservations cover all concurrent channels in the single service process until commit or failure. The service makes its final capacity check before it reads the preimage, so no rejection can depend on the outcome. If only the safe commission decreased, it reduces commission without changing the wager or increasing its reservation. If capacity is insufficient, it returns a signed rejection checkpoint. Commission is reported in the response and retained in the operator ledger; it is not an extra player debit or a wallet-verified fee commitment. The contract reserves no potential winnings. This is per-operation honest-operator risk admission, not collateral or a whole-hand reservation.

The preimage hashes to the bet's signed `roundHead`. Outcome is the low 64 bits of `keccak256(abi.encode(keccak256("HOOKEDIN/OUTCOME"), seed, preimage))`. **The stake is paid to enter, and every prize whose range holds the outcome pays**: `balance − amount + Σ payout` over the prizes with `rangeStart <= outcome < rangeEnd`. A prize is any non-empty range within `[0, 2^64)` with a positive payout below 2^128. Prizes may overlap, in which case they add; they need no order; an outcome in no prize pays nothing. One prize below a threshold is a coin-flip style wager, a prize per bucket is a Plinko board, a prize smaller than the stake is a partial loss, and a roulette player's chips are simply that player's prizes, overlapping where the chips do. The contract asks nothing more of a bet: whether it is worth taking is the casino's [admission](economics.md) decision, so covering the whole outcome space is legal and a bet that cannot lose is merely one the casino declines. The outcome depends only on the round and its seed, never on the channel or the rest of the operation, so every bet on one round head and seed sees the same 64-bit value. Transition hash is `keccak256(abi.encode(hashOperation,preimage))`.

An on-chain Step contains only the operation/authorization, disclosed preimage and casino signature over the derived checkpoint. `developer` in the signed operation is authoritative for the commission ledger; differing outer attribution is rejected. Evidence is either a joint checkpoint (or registered genesis), or that checkpoint plus one Step. The latter is independently usable even if the final player acknowledgment was lost. Games and their local allocations are outside the settlement protocol.

## Rounds

A round is one position of a private hash chain the casino keeps for an **address**, the round's **owner**: the signer key of a channel for that channel's own bets, or any key at all for whoever hosts rounds. An owner needs no channel and holds no money. It proves itself with `ChainAccess(address owner, uint256 expiresAt)` signed by that key, sent like a channel's `Access`. The head of a round is the hash of its position's unrevealed preimage: the chain root first, then each revealed preimage in turn. `POST /api/chains/:owner/round` returns `{owner, epoch, index, length, roundHead}`, creating the chain on first use (admitted at the rate of new channels) and moving to a fresh epoch when one is exhausted. A chain settles one round at a time, so a host that runs several rounds at once gives each its own key. A head needs no signature. Any head fixed before the seed is chosen is binding, because only its one preimage can settle an operation that signs it. The contract checks exactly that and knows nothing else about chains.

**Only the owner settles anything against its chain.** The usual round has one bet: a player sends its own bet to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations`, under the same key that owns its chain, naming its own next head and a seed its wallet drew after receiving that head. Neither side could choose the outcome: the casino committed first and the player's seed came second. Each settled bet reveals the head of the owner's next round, so the wallet asks for a head only for its first bet, after a rejection, and at the end of an epoch; an ordinary bet stays one request. If a remembered head has passed (a restored backup, a key that also hosts, a lost chain), the wallet withdraws the refused bet with a verified rejection kept under its own receipt, asks for the current head and signs the same bet once more under a new signed ID; the caller sees only the result.

A **hosted round** lets several channels share one outcome. The owner (a game's host, under a key of its own) announces its next head and a seed of its choosing. Each player signs a bet naming that head, that seed and its prizes, and gives `{request, signature, acknowledgment}` to the owner, which posts the list to `POST /api/chains/:owner/rounds` under its own authentication. Ranges express the game: equal ranges pay together, disjoint ranges never both pay, nested ranges pay in order. The owner signs no operation and stakes nothing.

The service locks every participating channel and the owner in sorted order, validates every entry, and admits the list as **one wager** (see [economics](economics.md#a-round-is-one-wager)). It then reads the owner's next preimage once, derives and signs each channel's checkpoint, and writes one signing-history record `{round: {owner, epoch, index}, responses}` before anything leaves the writer. That record also advances the owner's chain position, so a stale database restore replays the position from the log and can never open the same preimage twice; if the chain itself was lost the owner continues on a new epoch.

Admission is all-or-nothing. A malformed entry (bad signature, stale head, a different seed, insufficient balance, a channel that is closed or has not acknowledged its previous response, an operation already resolved) fails the request naming `bets[i]`, with nothing reserved or signed; the owner drops that seat and resubmits. Insufficient capacity returns a signed rejection checkpoint for **every** seat and opens no preimage. The casino can therefore never settle a subset chosen with knowledge of the outcome. A list whose operations all have recorded responses returns them: the exact retry. A round holds at most 32 bets, one per channel, all with one head and one seed, and at most 128 distinct total payouts across its outcome space.

A bet that reaches the casino from its own player for any round but that player's current one is refused with `Round head is not current`; nothing is signed. A player **withdraws** a signed bet nobody has settled by presenting it to `POST /api/channels/:id/cancel` as `{request, signature, acknowledgment}`: the casino returns the ordinary signed rejection above it, or the recorded result if the round's owner settled it first. A transfer offer nobody accepted is withdrawn the same way. The wallet saves a hosted bet as its pending operation, looks for its result with the receipt lookup, and never depends on the host for recovery.

Before any seat signs, the owner can ask `POST /api/chains/:owner/rounds/assess` with `{bets: [{stake, prizes}]}`. The reply `{admissible, commission | reason, bankroll}` is what admission would say to those terms at that moment: an unsigned planning hint like the bankroll figure, reserving nothing. A declined round costs every seat a rejection checkpoint and a new signature, so a host sizes its round with the hint first.

Trust in a hosted round differs from a player's own round and wallets say so: a game can place a hosted bet only after the player allows host-drawn randomness for that open game, and every hosted receipt is marked. The host cannot choose the outcome alone (it never knows the preimage) and the casino cannot alone (the seed follows its commitment), but **host and casino together can**. A game developer was already trusted for its rules; a hosted round extends that trust to the randomness of the atomic wager. A host can also withhold or delay a seat's bet, which costs the player nothing but the round. A seed taken from a public beacon would remove the collusion case with no protocol change, since the contract only ever sees `seed`.

## Tables

A table lets players play against **each other**, with the casino holding the money and a host they all named saying who is paid. It is how player-versus-player games settle without a developer holding funds.

```text
Table(address host, address developer, uint256 expiresAt, bytes32 nonce)
Settlement(bytes32 tableId, uint256 sequence, Payment[] payments, uint256 rake)
Payment(address player, uint256 amount)
```

The table ID is the EIP-712 hash of `Table`. A host is only a key: it holds no money and has no account at the casino, and a table exists at the casino from its first buy-in.

1. **Buy in.** A buy-in is a kind-4 transfer whose `counterparty` is the table ID, so one signature binds the host, the developer and the deadline. The player's own wallet sends it to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations` as `{request, signature, acknowledgment, table}`, where `table` is the terms; the casino checks that they hash to the counterparty. It completes at once, like an investment in the [bankroll fund](#the-bankroll-fund): the amount joins the table's pot in the casino's escrow and the channel is immediately free. Anyone may buy in until `expiresAt`, any number of times, at any number of tables. The host is not asked: it pays back whoever it does not seat. A buy-in to a table past its deadline returns the ordinary signed rejection checkpoint; the house risks nothing, so capacity never declines one. The host cannot buy into its own table, as a channel's player or as its signer.
2. **Settle.** The host signs `Settlement` and anyone posts it to `POST /api/tables/:id/settlements` as `{message, signature}`. Settlements are numbered from zero: the next one applies, the same one again returns the same answer, and any other is refused. The casino requires only that the table's host signed it, that it holds at most 32 payments, to distinct players and for positive amounts, that every paid player has bought into the table, and that payments plus rake do not exceed the pot. The host therefore shares the pot among the players who bought in however its game decides, which is what poker needs, but can never pay out more than came in, nor pay anyone who did not buy in. One record turns the payments into payouts owed and the rake into commission, split equally between the table's `developer` and the casino; an odd wei stays in the bankroll.
3. **Close.** At `expiresAt` the casino closes the table by itself and returns what is left of the pot to the players still owed their buy-in: in proportion to `max(0, bought − paid)`, rounded down (`closingPayments` in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts)). The rounding dust stays in the bankroll.
4. **Collect.** A payout is owed to the player's address, so it survives that player closing and reopening a channel. `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` lists what is due as `{source, index, amount, terms, settlement}`, where `source` is the table ID and `settlement` the host-signed `{message, signature}`, or null for a closing payment. The wallet recomputes the table ID from the terms. It then verifies the host's signature over a settlement that pays this player exactly that amount or, for an unsigned payout, requires that the table's deadline has passed. Only then does it sign the payout: a kind-5 credit whose `counterparty` is the table, for exactly that amount. Anything else is recorded as an alert on the table and never signed.

`GET /api/tables/:id` is public by ID, which only those at the table know. It returns `{id, terms, status, pot, sequence, seats: [{player, signer, bought, paid}]}`: `status` is `open` or `closed`, `sequence` is the next settlement the host may sign, and `signer` is the channel key that signed the player's latest buy-in.

The contract knows nothing of tables or pots: a buy-in and a payout are the two legs of a transfer whose other side is a table instead of a channel, and the casino attests that they belong together, exactly as it does for a transfer between channels.

**Trust.** Money on a table is the casino's promise: it has left the player's signed balance, is not protected principal, and no contract rule forces the casino to pay it out. The casino can never take more than the player bought in for, and a payout the host did not sign is refused by the wallet until the deadline has passed. The amount of a closing payment is the casino's word. The host can decide wrongly, but only among the players who bought in and never for more than the pot. The accounting bankroll excludes the [escrow](economics.md#available-capital-and-concurrency): every table's pot and every payout awarded but not yet collected.

**Identity.** A game's own server learns who is playing from a token the wallet signs with the channel key:

```text
Identity(bytes32 channelId, string origin, bytes32 nonce, uint256 expiresAt)
```

The server issues the nonce. The wallet states the origin itself, from the entry URL of the game page it loaded, so a token obtained by one game is useless at another game's server. The token is valid for five minutes and authorizes nothing at the casino. The server checks the origin, nonce and expiry, recovers the signing key (`identitySigner` in [protocol/protocol.ts](../protocol/protocol.ts)) and matches it to the `signer` the casino reports for a seat of its table.

## The bankroll fund

Anyone with a channel can **invest in the bankroll** and later divest. An investor trusts the casino completely. A share is the casino's signed promise of a part of the bankroll: no contract holds it, the casino alone states what the bankroll is worth, and the bankroll can lose. What the protocol gives the investor is proof of what they hold, and the casino no way to deny it.

```text
ShareStatement(address holder, uint256 sequence, uint256 shares, uint256 amount, uint256 equity, uint256 totalShares, bytes32 cause)
Redeem(address holder, uint256 shares, uint256 sequence)
Fund(uint256 sequence, uint256 totalShares, uint256 houseShares, uint256 equity, uint256 overdrawn, uint256 at)
```

The fund is a transfer counterparty, like a table: `FUND_ID = keccak256("HOOKEDIN/BANKROLL")`. **Investing** is a kind-4 transfer that names it, sent to `POST /api/channels/:id/operations` like a payment. No ETH moves: the signed balance falls, so the accounting bankroll, and with it what the casino can cover, rises by the same amount. **Equity** is that bankroll before in-flight reservations. The price is fixed before anything is awaited: `amount × totalShares / equity` shares, rounded down, are minted to the channel's **player address**, so a holding outlives any one channel. With the first investment the bankroll the house had already built becomes the house's own shares, one per wei. The response carries the casino's `ShareStatement`: the holder's shares afterwards, the amount, the `equity` and `totalShares` just before that fixed the price, and as `cause` the hash of the holder's signed transfer. One signing-history record holds the step and the fund change. An amount too small for a share, or a bankroll with nothing left, is declined with the ordinary signed rejection.

**Divesting** starts with the holder's `Redeem`, signed by the key of an open channel of that player and posted to `POST /api/channels/:id/fund/redeem`. `sequence` is the statement it will produce, so it works once; asking again returns the recorded statement. The shares are burned for `shares × equity / totalShares`, rounded down, which leaves the bankroll at once and is owed to the player exactly as a table's payout is: listed by `GET /api/channels/:id/payouts` under `FUND_ID` and collected with a kind-5 credit for exactly that amount. A redemption larger than the unreserved bankroll is refused until the bets counting on that money settle. Minting and burning at the going price leave every other share worth what it was.

A holder's evidence is their highest-numbered statement; the casino's only answer to a smaller holding is a later `Redeem` the holder signed. The wallet checks each statement's signature, that it follows its last one, that its cause is the exact transfer or redemption it signed, and that the shares and amount are what the stated price implies. It signs for redeemed money only in an amount one of its statements priced. It cannot check `equity` or `totalShares` themselves: players' balances are private, so the price is the casino's word. `GET /api/fund` returns that word signed and timestamped, a quote the casino can be held to, and `GET /api/channels/:id/fund` the player's holding with its statement.

**The owner's capital.** Everything the chain can show moves pool cash and a liability together (deposits, closures, claims, developer payouts), except the owner adding to the pool or withdrawing from it. At each committed observation any such change in equity is the house's: it is issued, or gives up, shares at the price just before, in a signing-history record of its own, so the price does not move. The house cannot give up shares it does not have. That excess is a loss every holder bears, and is reported as `overdrawn` from then on. While an investor's channel is open the contract still protects their original deposit, so the owner cannot withdraw it; that protection ends with the channel, and the shares remain a promise. Owner capital that moves while the service is down with its database lost is not attributed.

## Fast exchange

1. Wallet saves the exact request and channel-key signature. The request carries no checkpoint signature: the casino never holds a player-signed checkpoint above a wager it has not yet decided.
2. Casino requires acknowledgment of its previous response, validates the authorization and exact terms, and serializes the channel position.
3. Casino commits either the signed result or its signature over an unchanged-balance rejection checkpoint at n+2 to its signing history and result store before sending it. Commission accrues only on an executed bet.
4. Wallet verifies the result or rejection, countersigns the new checkpoint, and saves the evidence, receipt and cleared pending operation together before returning to the game. The countersignature travels as the acknowledgment of the next request.

The rejection checkpoint retains the base balance. Its `previousStateHash` identifies the base and its `transitionHash` is the exact rejected operation digest. No preimage is disclosed or consumed. Once the wallet countersigns it, the existing contract supports it without a new transition kind; until then the jointly signed base settles to the identical balance, so the casino defends with the base and loses nothing. After saving rejection n+2, the wallet can submit a new bet at n+3. The higher checkpoint can challenge an attempted settlement of the rejected n+1 wager; the player must still meet the existing challenge deadline. Because the player signs the rejection only after receiving it, a completed result at n+1 can never be superseded by a rejection the casino signs later.

The next request can carry acknowledgment. Retrying an operation ID returns the same accepted or rejected receipt; a new attempt needs a new ID. Only a verified joint rejection permits the wallet to clear a rejected signed wager. An HTTP error or timeout leaves it unresolved: recover the exact request or close. Previously committed results remain recoverable during stale chain observations, even when new admission is paused. The wallet requests its round head before generating each bet's seed.

An exhausted chain is no longer secret. `GET /api/chains/:owner/:epoch` is public and returns the seed and length of any epoch below the owner's current one. The wallet keeps each rejected bet's `{owner, epoch, index}`, regenerates the chain, checks the preimage against the rejected wager's signed round head, and records what that bet would have paid, on its own rounds and on hosted ones alike. A casino that declines wagers by outcome is therefore detectable by its players whenever a chain is retired, without any protocol penalty.

Unsigned bankroll information in responses and `/api/metrics` is a planning hint, not an admission promise. The wallet sends the selected wager directly; the service accepts it or returns a signed rejection. Deploy the contract, wallet and service from the same prototype revision.

## Closing

`cooperativeClose(evidence, playerCloseSignature, casinoCloseSignature)` uses `Close(channelId,stateHash)` signed by the original funding wallet and casino. `startClose(evidence)` is available to the player or casino. `challengeClose(evidence)` accepts strictly newer supported sequences from any submitter before the deadline; re-submitting the proposed sequence reverts. Channel status and the uint64 deadline share a storage slot with the player address; a timestamp that cannot represent the full challenge period is rejected. Challenges never extend the deadline. Cooperative close also works during a pending unilateral close, before its deadline, provided the state is at least as new as the proposed closing state and agrees at an equal sequence. At the exact deadline, challenges and cooperative close stop and `finalizeClose(channelId)` becomes available.

Finalization establishes a claim without making any external payment. Collection is a separate transaction. It consumes the channel once, replaces its open principal reservation with min(deposit,balance), and records winnings=max(balance-deposit,0). `claim` pays protected principal and allocated winnings without new signatures; rejection reverts only this collection. Paid totals are computed as amount minus remaining principal and winnings. `Step` contains the operation, authorization, preimage and casino signature; the next checkpoint is derived, never transmitted in the proof. A missing/withheld result does not create a penalty or debt beyond the latest supported completed state.

Deposits and supported balances are below `MAX_BALANCE = 2^128` wei, so the uint256 aggregate `unpaidWinnings` cannot overflow through any number of independently valid claims and block another player's finalization.

## Settlement priority and authority

The deployer is the immutable casino owner and settlement signer. The constructor takes no arguments. Only the owner can call `withdrawHouse(withdrawalId, recipient, amount)`. It rejects zero/reused IDs, zero amounts, the zero address and the casino contract as recipients, and amounts exceeding withdrawal headroom. A successful withdrawal consumes its ID and emits `HouseWithdrawal(withdrawalId, recipient, amount)`. A failed transfer reverts the ID as well. Developer cash payouts use their stable payout ID through this same function. There is no treasury role or signed Withdrawal relay.

Every signature the contract checks is an EOA signature: channel keys, the casino, and the funding account's close authorization. A contract account that funds a channel exits through `startClose`/`finalizeClose`.

The owner is trusted with all house liquidity: it can create jointly signed checkpoints for its own channels and redeem those claims. A second withdrawal key would not stop that. Users must check their wallet and challenge any stale close before the fixed 24-hour deadline. If they miss it, a previously signed lower balance can become final. An independent watchtower is optional.

Finalized positive winnings enter a FIFO queue. Funding and claims allocate at most eight queue entries per call; `allocateWinnings(limit)` processes up to 64. Allocation reserves ETH before attempting transfers. A rejecting senior recipient retains its allocation while juniors can receive later money. A beneficiary can call `claimTo(channelId, recipient)`; strangers can only trigger payment to its saved destination.

`houseCash = contract balance - protectedPrincipal - reservedWinnings`. Owner withdrawal headroom is `max(0, balance - protectedPrincipal - total unpaid winnings)`; allocated winnings are already part of that debt. A malicious settlement signer can fabricate claims, but cannot use a junior claim to consume cash ahead of already-finalized senior debt. Active private winnings remain unsecured shared-pool promises.

## Transfers

The sender signs kind 4 for an exact amount and recipient channel. The service reserves that signing position and records the offer in the signing history before projecting it into the sender's channel row, so a stale database restore recovers it. A pending response changes neither balance and is not settlement evidence. Until the recipient accepts, the sender can cancel: `POST /api/channels/:id/cancel` returns the casino's signature over the rejection checkpoint at the following sequence, the wallet countersigns it, the offer is withdrawn, and the channel continues at the next sequence. The recipient accepts by signing kind 5 at its own next channel position. This receive authorization is saved before submission, so an incoming transfer cannot overtake a locally signed wager. Both channels must remain open; self-transfers are rejected.

The service verifies the matching sender, recipient, amount and outgoing digest, locks both channels in sorted order, and produces both casino-signed results. One mirrored signing-log record retains both responses before one database transaction commits both balances. No commission, hash-chain preimage or additional bankroll reservation is involved. The total signed balance is conserved. Exact retries recover the committed result, including after a stale database restore; a mismatched restore stops new signing. An uncompleted offer is replayed from the signing history into a restored database, and the history still fences its position.

Each leg is ordinary base-plus-one-step evidence. The recipient authorizes receiving before completion but need not acknowledge the resulting checkpoint for that evidence to settle. Each checkpoint binds its counterparty; the credit's operation ID commits to the complete outgoing authorization. The contract checks each leg's signatures and arithmetic independently. The trusted casino attests that a matching debit exists; the contract does not enforce atomic settlement or conservation across channels. The casino already has authority to issue arbitrary jointly accepted balances.

Transfers retain the one-pending-operation limit. A recipient must be available and have no signed operation before accepting. An unavailable recipient or opposing pending transfers are resolved by the sender cancelling; there is no timeout or automatic refund. Game developers must coordinate readiness and wait for a verified receipt before treating a payment as complete.

Protected principal remains tied to each original deposit. Transferred balances above a recipient's deposit are shared-pool winnings, with the same FIFO rules and liquidity risk as other winnings. Transfers do not move protected principal or create collateral.
