# Independent wallet

The wallet opens a persistent channel directly on-chain with one deposit and channel-key registration transaction, without contacting the casino first. The deposit requires two confirmations on Sepolia or one on Anvil. The wallet learns its next round head from the casino, or from the preimage its last bet revealed, and only then draws the bet's seed. A game may bet on a [hosted round](protocol.md#rounds), whose seed is its host's, only after the player allows it in the wallet's dialog for that open game; the wallet persists nothing about that choice and marks each such receipt. A local channel key signs atomic wagers within the open game's spending limit; the funding wallet signs deposits and closes. Bets and deterministic game payments need no gas or wallet-provider call.

IndexedDB stores locally held funding/channel keys, signed checkpoints, requests, receipts and transaction intents. Nothing about games is persisted: a game's spending limit lives in the open tab's memory and games keep their own state at their own origin. An injected wallet retains its funding key externally. Small settings use localStorage. The wallet persists each request before sending it, then commits the verified result before showing success. An uncertain response remains pending and must be retried exactly or resolved by closure. Web Locks and persisted revisions serialize competing browser tabs.

Browser funding keys are retained by address, with a separate selected-account pointer. A chain-scoped Web Lock and atomic IndexedDB update make concurrent first startup select one durable account before displaying its receiving address. Selecting or importing another account never erases retained keys; use **Saved browser wallets** to switch back. Encrypted backups cover only the selected account: its locally held funding key and channel evidence. Back up each funded account separately to retain all of their channel records; a key alone does not reconstruct the latest off-chain state.

With a trusted deployment, an unavailable casino does not prevent a deposit or unilateral recovery; playing requires reconnection. The wallet's single background loop checks chain status every four seconds, including reconnect, then polls for native transfers and audits retired rejections; the page only re-renders from wallet state. An unchanged observation does not write a new durable revision, so it never invalidates another tab's action. On Sepolia, it corroborates the confirmed block hash with a separately configured witness RPC, rejects stale/future timestamps, and checks confirmed height/hash progression. Successful polling of a frozen chain does not renew its progression deadline. Failed observations immediately pause new play. Local Anvil explicitly exempts timestamp/stall checks because it mines on demand. Unsigned bankroll hints arrive in responses and background metrics requests; a wager uses one direct operation request. The same channel is usable across games and visits. Additional deposits require an explicit close and reopen.

Every core poll includes the canonical active channel, selected channel and any explicit recovery target. A separate optional refresh reads up to 16 historical channels and the retained activity, using at most eight concurrent RPC tasks, and fetches bankroll information outside the action lock. Optional results merge into the latest durable revision under the existing lock, updating only unchanged historical entries for the same account. Bankroll information commits independently of activity; a newer hint is preserved. Failures display stale activity without invalidating a fresh active-channel observation. HTTP RPC requests time out after ten seconds, releasing a failed core refresh before another action proceeds. This preserves prompt stale-close detection when a reorg restores an old active channel while bounding historical RPC bursts. Claim collection, challenge and finalization explicitly refresh their target. Historical claims display their observation time and may lag by a full batch sweep; with 1,000 retained channels, that is roughly 252 seconds at the normal poll interval. The claim list initially shows 20 rows, prioritizing unpaid claims. Evidence is never deleted by batching or pagination.

## Display and recovery

Playing balance, original protected deposit, signed winnings awaiting cash-out, finalized withdrawal claims, and actual ETH received are shown separately. A losing channel receives only its remaining balance; principal protection does not refund losses.

Export/import signed recovery evidence from the wallet. Recovery bundles contain the on-chain opening identity and minimal settlement evidence, with no private keys or game data. Importing newer financial evidence clears local game allocations and checkpoints because the bundle cannot attribute balance changes to games. Encrypted wallet backups preserve game progress and keys. Without the channel key, imported evidence still supports independent closing and claims. Keep a copy of the funding wallet and enough ETH for gas.

Evidence export and encrypted backup wait for the shared channel lock and read the latest durable record, including a checkpoint saved by another tab while waiting. The default channel is resolved from that record. Export confirmation shows the channel and sequence; neither export requires a casino or chain request.

Cooperative closure establishes the claim immediately. Use Collect claim afterwards to receive available principal and allocated winnings; closing itself transfers no ETH. Otherwise start unilateral closure, monitor its exact 24-hour deadline, challenge newer evidence before that deadline, and finalize afterwards. The wallet shows old unpaid claims after another channel opens. Claim collection can be repeated after replenishment without casino cooperation. A reorged closure reselects known active-channel evidence and preserves any previously signed close authorization; play stays stopped. If a deposit disappears, recovery can resubmit its original opening without discarding newer signed evidence.

Pending on-chain transactions expose **Recover** and **Speed up transaction**. Normal confirmation, replacement and recovery all corroborate the canonical receipt and its intended action before atomically clearing the saved intent. Opening activation separately corroborates the registered channel against the saved opening identity. A failed check retains recovery data and does not renew readiness.

Recovery checks the canonical transaction that consumed the saved funding-account nonce, including an external wallet's replacement after a restart. Matching replacements complete the saved action; cancellation, different actions and reverts clear the intent with a zero-payment failure record. If an injected submission returned no hash and its nonce remains unresolved, **Recover** requests approval to resend the exact saved nonce, recipient, value and calldata on the same chain. Rejecting that retry leaves the uncertain original intact. Speed-up uses the same path, raises fees within a 200 gwei cap, and persists replacement attempts.

Activity retains receipt block hashes and original transaction details. Each optional refresh checks at most the 100 retained activity entries, sharing block reads for transactions mined together and refetching receipts only when their branch changed. Removed receipts are marked unconfirmed with zero confirmed ETH received; re-inclusion updates their receipt and payment amount. Orphaned entries explicitly direct the user to refresh or retry using the saved transaction details in their funding wallet. Historical actions are not automatically rebroadcast. A confirmed settlement with zero ETH received may still have an unpaid claim, shown separately below.

Wallet history and the live game log share the same expandable entry, timestamp, search and JSON-copy controls. Wallet summaries show exact ETH amounts, settlement status, stake and signed balance; expanded receipts expose operation/channel IDs, sequence, developer, commission and transaction links. Search matches all displayed details and JSON, including collapsed payloads. Background refresh preserves unchanged rows and keeps updated receipts expanded. Copied game payload previews are explicitly marked when truncated; saved receipt JSON is complete.

Initial transactions and speed-ups are limited to 2 million gas, 200 gwei per gas, and 0.05 ETH maximum total gas fee. RPC estimates exceeding these release-owned limits fail before signing; casino configuration cannot raise them. Deposits retain the emergency gas reserve. During unusually expensive network conditions, use the independent recovery CLI with a deliberately reviewed fee policy.

## Game boundary

Every page has a URL: `/` (library), `/wallet`, `/activity`, `/games/<id>` for a catalog game with a manifest `id`, and `/games/custom?manifest=<url>` for any other manifest. The static host serves the client page for these routes; the client loads the manifest after the wallet starts. The library is [catalog.json](../catalog.json), a list of absolute manifest URLs read from the configured `games` base URL; each listed game is its own site on its own origin. Opening a game link grants no spending authority. Games load and render without an open channel: the iframe sees a zero, disabled balance, wagers fail with a message, and a funding request opens the wallet's dialog in a mode that points to My wallet instead of an amount. The allocation is created the first time a channel is open, so a game opened before the wallet finished starting attaches to the channel once it appears. A link to a game the connected library does not list returns to the library with an explanation.

An open game has the whole page under the top bar: the play page draws no heading, frame or footer, and the top bar's Games link leads back. The deposit dialog, the one place a game is given money, names the host the game is served from and the developer that earns its commission. Opening the wallet with `?log` in the URL adds a live developer log below the game. Each entry is numbered and shows the gap since the previous entry. Bridge requests are summarized in one line (a bet's stake, number of prizes, the most they can pay together and its exact return to player, or the amount and reason of a funding request); replies are paired with their request and show the round-trip time and outcome (result, balance, rejection reason, or decline). Balance pushes, wallet progress messages, client actions and validation failures are logged with a badge, and a filter shows all events, bridge traffic only, wallet events only or errors only. Export JSON downloads the retained entries with complete payloads. It retains the latest 500 events in memory for the current view; Clear or opening another game resets it. Payload display is capped at 70,000 characters. This is a session diagnostic, not persisted wallet history, and does not capture the isolated iframe's internal console or network traffic.

Game iframes run with `sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin"`: they keep their host's origin and storage, and receive the generic atomic API, public wallet information, their spending limit and verified receipts. Each game implements its own rules, sampling and persistence. The wallet checks signatures, entropy, balance changes and rejection checkpoints, without interpreting game state. Games cannot access keys, future or pending entropy, funding transactions or the wallet's storage, and cannot request arbitrary signatures. The wallet refuses a manifest or entry on its own origin, and its host sends `frame-ancestors 'none'` so wallet pages cannot be framed at all; any deployment must keep that header. Manifest URL, entry URL and developer identify the game and scope its operation IDs. Assets at those URLs are not content-pinned.

Only the wallet's deposit dialog grants spending authority. A game can ask for money (`game.requestFunds`) with a suggested amount and a reason; the wallet shows them in that dialog, where the player chooses the amount or declines, and replies to the game with the decision. The authorized amount is a spending limit held only in memory for the open tab. Leaving the game, reloading or closing the tab releases it; the money never left the channel balance, and the game asks again next time. The play page shows no wallet controls: the game presents its own balance, and recovering a pending operation happens through the pending banner. Only one game per wallet can hold a limit at a time across tabs; a second tab is refused at the funding dialog. The wallet pushes the game's limit and pending state into the iframe whenever they change. The game can risk that limit, including winnings. A result recovered after a reload changes the channel balance only. Games cannot add money or alter their fee recipient. The wallet does not certify advertised rules or whole-game outcomes. See the [game SDK](https://github.com/hookedin/game-sdk/blob/main/docs/game-sdk.md).

## Trust and persistence

The client ships its ABI/runtime ([client/contract-artifact.ts](../client/contract-artifact.ts)) and pins a deployment manifest independently of the casino. The manifest includes `chainId`, `contractAddress`, `operator`, `rpcUrl`, and `witnessRpcUrl` on Sepolia. It is the `deployment` field of the wallet's configuration, which the [build](#build-and-serving) publishes as `config.js`. Publish that configuration through the trusted wallet release process. A fresh browser bootstraps recovery from this manifest when the casino API is offline, returns errors or malformed data, or advertises a different chain, contract, operator or protocol. Recovery mode disables new play and cooperative closure, while preserving import/export and unilateral settlement. Import the funding key or connect its wallet, then import settlement evidence. Runtime, owner, network and RPC checks still apply. Reload after the compatible service returns.

The wallet's origin serves only the wallet. Games, and the SDK code they bundle, are served by their own hosts and run inside game iframes; the wallet's CSP restricts scripts to its own origin.

Accepted settlement state, the compact signed receipt, and clearing of pending state commit together. Invalid optional bankroll information is discarded. A bet or transfer request carries only the signed operation and the wallet's acknowledgment of its previous checkpoint. To decline, the casino signs an unchanged-balance rejection checkpoint one sequence above the operation; the wallet verifies that signature over the exact saved request, countersigns it and saves it with the rejection receipt before another operation is permitted. The countersignature travels as the acknowledgment of the next request. A timeout or generic HTTP error remains pending. Rejections consume no balance or entropy. A retry uses a new operation ID above the saved rejection, while exact retries of the rejected ID recover its original receipt. Wallet settings provide authenticated encrypted backups (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 iterations, AES-256-GCM with random salt/IV); the browser funding key, channel keys and pending operations are included. External funding keys stay in their funding wallet. No game state is included. Backup restore and funding-account selection use the same Web Lock and durable revision checks as gameplay. Restore rejects evidence downgrades, same-sequence conflicts and outstanding local intents against the latest persisted record, including changes made by another tab while decrypting. Existing close authorizations remain fenced from further play.

Recovery transactions can spend the reserved gas amount; deposits still preserve it. `claimTo` is available through the claim recipient field. Permissionless challenge/finalize/claim calls can also use a separately funded CLI relayer. Contract funding wallets must expose compatible typed-data signing and transaction submission; historical channel signing remains an EOA key.

## Build and serving

`npm run build` compiles the contract, checks it against the pinned artifact, and runs [scripts/build.ts](../scripts/build.ts), which writes the whole site to `dist/`:

| File                      | Contents                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `main.js`, `main.js.map`  | Every wallet and protocol module, bundled by esbuild from [client/main.ts](../client/main.ts) into one readable, unminified ES module        |
| `vendor/ethers.js`        | Copied byte for byte from the installed npm release of ethers (`dist/ethers.min.js`), so its hash can be compared with the published package |
| `config.js`               | `export default { network, casino, games, deployment? }`: this deployment's settings                                                         |
| `index.html`, `style.css` | Copied from `client/`                                                                                                                        |
| `brand/`, `catalog.json`  | The mark and the list of games                                                                                                               |
| `_headers`                | The response headers Cloudflare applies, from [client/\_headers](../client/_headers)                                                         |

`main.js` imports exactly `/config.js` and `/vendor/ethers.js`; `test/static.test.ts` checks that, the ethers hash and the headers. `config.js` is the JSON file named by the `HOOKEDIN_CLIENT_CONFIG` environment variable at build time, or the defaults in [client/config.ts](../client/config.ts) without it. `network` is `sepolia` or `local`, `casino` is the casino API's base URL, `games` is the base URL that serves `catalog.json`, and `deployment` is the pinned manifest above. Settings saved in the wallet override `network`, `casino` and `games` in that browser.

`npm run dev` builds and serves `dist/` at `http://127.0.0.1:4184` (`PORT` overrides) through [scripts/static.ts](../scripts/static.ts), which imitates the production host: it applies `_headers`, answers every extensionless route with the page, and refuses dotfiles and anything outside `dist/`. Production is a Cloudflare static-assets Worker ([wrangler.jsonc](../wrangler.jsonc)): `npm run build`, then `wrangler deploy` publishes `dist/` with the single-page fallback turned on. The headers set `script-src 'self'` and `frame-ancestors 'none'`; any other host must send the same ones.

## Casino authority and manual challenges

One casino owner controls signing and house withdrawals. Winnings are not protected from that owner or guaranteed payable. Principal protection applies to the state accepted at closure; the casino can propose an older signed state, and missing its challenge deadline can reduce the amount returned.

Check the wallet regularly while a channel is open, leaving enough time to mine a challenge within 24 hours of a closure starting. The wallet shows a persistent warning and a **Challenge stale close** button when its saved checkpoint is newer than the proposed closure. Opening the wallet or seeing the warning does not submit a transaction: the user must press the button and get the challenge mined before the deadline. Keep current evidence and ETH for gas. A watchtower is optional.

## Rejected wagers

Every bet receipt records what the player signed and what happened: the stake, the round's 64-bit outcome, what the prizes paid, the most they could have paid together and the bet's exact expected payout, so the activity list shows each bet's return to player to a hundredth of a basis point. The wallet computes these from the signed prizes, never from anything the game says.

A rejected bet keeps its receipt. When the wallet's background loop next runs after that round's chain is exhausted, it fetches the retired chain's seed from the casino, checks it against the rejection's signed round head, and records on the receipt what the declined wager would have paid. A run of rejections that would all have paid well is the player's evidence of selective rejection.

## Native transfers

The wallet polls for native transfers when idle, first recovering its own pending transfer or accepting one incoming credit from the developer of a game the player has funded. Unrelated senders need explicit acceptance through the wallet API; their unsolicited offers cannot occupy the channel automatically. The wallet saves the receive authorization before submission and verifies the resulting native step before crediting unallocated balance. It never replaces a pending wager with an incoming payment. A pending outgoing transfer shows a **Cancel transfer** action: the casino signs the rejection checkpoint above the transfer, the wallet verifies and countersigns it, the balance is unchanged and play continues at the next sequence. Returned game payouts do not replenish allocations or re-enable stopped games. The transfer receipt remains usable through the existing recovery and closure paths; the game developer controls the actual payout decision.

## Compact recovery backups

Encrypted backups use `HOOKEDIN/WALLET/1` inside `HOOKEDIN/WALLET-BACKUP/1`. They include only the selected account's locally held funding key, channel keys, latest signed evidence, close authorizations, and exact unresolved operation/transaction intents. Up to 100 recent compact receipts appear once.

Backups contain no game state. They have a 16 MiB limit and depend on browser storage availability. They do not contain other accounts or a game replay archive.

A failed commit blocks further actions until durable restoration. The wallet displays the last verified observation, saved/proposed sequence, challenge deadline, pending challenge, protected remaining principal and allocated winnings. The gas reserve is a floor, not a guaranteed challenge budget.

Execution retries must repeat the original kind, amount, every prize and the developer. Both cached and pending operations reject changed terms. A game names its operations with its own IDs, which the wallet scopes by channel and game, so a game can look up an outcome after a reload with `game.receipt`. Use `wallet.getReceipt(operationId)` to retrieve a saved receipt without executing. A persistence failure blocks further wallet actions, including actions waiting on a refresh; reload from durable storage before continuing.
