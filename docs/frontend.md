# Independent wallet

The wallet opens a persistent channel directly on-chain with one deposit and channel-key registration transaction, without contacting the casino first. The deposit requires two confirmations on Sepolia or one on Anvil. The wallet learns its next round from the casino, which names it in the reply to the last bet, and only then draws the bet's seed; the bet signs the seed's hash and the seed travels with it. A game may bet on a [hosted round](protocol.md#rounds), whose seed is its host's and stays with the host until the round closes, only when its manifest declares that it runs them; the wallet asks the player before it frames such a game and does not open it at all if they refuse, persists nothing about that choice, and marks each such receipt. No game states what it pays back, and the manifest has no field for it: nothing bounds how often a game wagers the money it holds, so a promised percentage is unverifiable. The wallet computes each bet's exact return from its own prize table before it signs, and keeps that figure with the receipt, where the player reads it. A local channel key signs atomic wagers within the open game's spending limit; the funding wallet signs deposits and closes. Bets and deterministic game payments need no gas or wallet-provider call.

IndexedDB stores locally held funding/channel keys, signed checkpoints, requests, receipts and transaction intents. Nothing about games is persisted: a game's spending limit lives in the open tab's memory and games keep their own state at their own origin. An injected wallet retains its funding key externally. Small settings use localStorage. The wallet persists each request before sending it, then commits the verified result before showing success. An uncertain response remains pending and must be retried exactly or resolved by closure. Web Locks and persisted revisions serialize competing browser tabs.

Browser funding keys are retained by address, with a separate selected-account pointer. A chain-scoped Web Lock and atomic IndexedDB update make concurrent first startup select one durable account before displaying its receiving address. Selecting or importing another account never erases retained keys; use **Saved browser wallets** to switch back. Encrypted backups cover only the selected account: its locally held funding key and channel evidence. Back up each funded account separately to retain all of their channel records; a key alone does not reconstruct the latest off-chain state.

With a trusted deployment, an unavailable casino does not prevent a deposit or unilateral recovery; playing requires reconnection. The wallet's single background loop checks chain status every four seconds, including reconnect, then polls for native transfers and audits retired rejections; the page only re-renders from wallet state. An unchanged observation does not write a new durable revision, so it never invalidates another tab's action. On Sepolia, it corroborates the confirmed block hash with a separately configured witness RPC, rejects stale/future timestamps, and checks confirmed height/hash progression. Successful polling of a frozen chain does not renew its progression deadline. Failed observations immediately pause new play. Local Anvil explicitly exempts timestamp/stall checks because it mines on demand. Unsigned bankroll hints arrive in responses and background metrics requests; a wager uses one direct operation request. The same channel is usable across games and visits. Additional deposits require an explicit close and reopen.

Every core poll includes the canonical active channel, selected channel and any explicit recovery target. A separate optional refresh reads up to 16 historical channels and the retained activity, using at most eight concurrent RPC tasks, and fetches bankroll information outside the action lock. Optional results merge into the latest durable revision under the existing lock, updating only unchanged historical entries for the same account. Bankroll information commits independently of activity; a newer hint is preserved. Failures display stale activity without invalidating a fresh active-channel observation. HTTP RPC requests time out after ten seconds, releasing a failed core refresh before another action proceeds. This preserves prompt stale-close detection when a reorg restores an old active channel while bounding historical RPC bursts. Claim collection, challenge and finalization explicitly refresh their target. Historical claims display their observation time and may lag by a full batch sweep; with 1,000 retained channels, that is roughly 252 seconds at the normal poll interval. The claim list initially shows 20 rows, prioritizing unpaid claims. Evidence is never deleted by batching or pagination.

## Display and recovery

Playing balance, original protected deposit, signed winnings awaiting cash-out, finalized withdrawal claims, and actual ETH received are shown separately. A losing channel receives only its remaining balance; principal protection does not refund losses. The test-coin balance stands beside them, with a button that claims 100 coins from the faucet when it has run low. When this account's games have earned commission, the playing balance says how much they have earned and how much of it is collected; the background loop collects it into the channel, and the receipt reads **Developer earnings**.

My account holds the names this wallet answers to, and My games the games it publishes. Every player has a **uname** the casino derives, twenty-four characters written `~3byt9ocwnnzaxanmiz3stocj`; from a funded ETH channel they may also take an **alias**, up to twenty ASCII letters, digits and underscores written `@Bob`, and give it up again. Both always find the player, and two aliases that read alike are the same alias, so `@Bob` and `@B0b` cannot both exist. The card also publishes games, up to a hundred, each a name and a manifest URL: `@Bob/game`, or `~uname/game` without an alias, is the wallet URL that opens it, and anyone can play it. A page shows the first 32 of a profile's games, because every one of them is a manifest its owner chose and the page fetches each; the rest stay reachable by their own URL. A player and their public record are read from `/api/players/<name>`, the route everyone reads them from.

Export/import signed recovery evidence from the wallet. Recovery bundles contain the on-chain opening identity and minimal settlement evidence, with no private keys or game data. Importing evidence rejects downgrades, same-sequence conflicts, outstanding local intents, another deployment or player, and an unregistered opening, then writes that channel's checkpoint. Encrypted wallet backups preserve locally held keys and channel records. Without the channel key, imported evidence still supports independent closing and claims. Keep a copy of the funding wallet and enough ETH for gas.

Evidence export and encrypted backup wait for the shared channel lock and read the latest durable record, including a checkpoint saved by another tab while waiting. The default channel is resolved from that record. Export confirmation shows the channel and sequence; neither export requires a casino or chain request.

Cooperative closure establishes the claim immediately. Use **Collect available funds** afterwards to receive available principal and allocated winnings; closing itself transfers no ETH. Otherwise start unilateral closure, monitor its exact 24-hour deadline, challenge newer evidence before that deadline, and finalize afterwards. The wallet shows old unpaid claims after another channel opens. Claim collection can be repeated after replenishment without casino cooperation. A reorged closure reselects known active-channel evidence and preserves a close authorization already signed; play stays stopped. If a deposit disappears, recovery can resubmit its original opening without discarding newer signed evidence.

Pending on-chain transactions expose **Retry same request** and **Speed up transaction**. Normal confirmation, replacement and recovery all corroborate the canonical receipt and its intended action before atomically clearing the saved intent. Opening activation separately corroborates the registered channel against the saved opening identity. A failed check retains recovery data and does not renew readiness.

Recovery checks the canonical transaction that consumed the saved funding-account nonce, including an external wallet's replacement after a restart. Matching replacements complete the saved action; cancellation, different actions and reverts clear the intent with a zero-payment failure record. If an injected submission returned no hash and its nonce remains unresolved, **Retry same request** requests approval to resend the exact saved nonce, recipient, value and calldata on the same chain. Rejecting that retry leaves the uncertain original intact. Speed-up uses the same path, raises fees within a 200 gwei cap, and persists replacement attempts.

Activity retains receipt block hashes and original transaction details. Each optional refresh checks at most the 100 retained activity entries, sharing block reads for transactions mined together and refetching receipts only when their branch changed. Removed receipts are marked unconfirmed with zero confirmed ETH received; re-inclusion updates their receipt and payment amount. Orphaned entries explicitly direct the user to refresh or retry using the saved transaction details in their funding wallet. Historical actions are not automatically rebroadcast. A confirmed settlement with zero ETH received may still have an unpaid claim, shown separately below.

Wallet history and the live game log share the same expandable entry, timestamp, search and JSON-copy controls. Wallet summaries show exact ETH amounts, settlement status, stake and signed balance; expanded receipts expose operation/channel IDs, sequence, developer, commission and transaction links. Search matches all displayed details and JSON, including collapsed payloads. Background refresh preserves unchanged rows and keeps updated receipts expanded. Copied game payload previews are explicitly marked when truncated; saved receipt JSON is complete.

Initial transactions and speed-ups are limited to 2 million gas, 200 gwei per gas, and 0.05 ETH maximum total gas fee. RPC estimates exceeding these release-owned limits fail before signing; casino configuration cannot raise them. Deposits retain the emergency gas reserve. During unusually expensive network conditions, use the independent recovery CLI with a deliberately reviewed fee policy.

## Game boundary

Every page has a URL: `/` (library), `/account`, and under it `/wallet` (balances, deposit address, channel and claims), `/games` (what this wallet has played, its favourites and what it publishes), `/bets` (every bet it signed), `/bankroll`, `/settings` and `/activity`; `/games/<key>` for a game's public record, every bet anyone has placed in it; `/@<alias>` or `/~<uname>` for a player's public page, the same with `/<game>` for a game published there, and `/games/custom?manifest=<url>` for any other manifest. The static host serves the client page for these routes; the client loads the manifest after the wallet starts. A game is a manifest URL somebody publishes in their profile at the casino, up to a hundred each; the library is what `@hookedin` publishes and what this account publishes itself, and each game is its own site on its own origin. Opening a game link grants no spending authority. Games load and render without an open channel: the iframe sees a zero balance, wagers fail with a message, and a funding request opens the wallet's dialog in a mode that offers test coins or My wallet instead of an amount. The allocation is created the first time a channel is open, so a game opened before the wallet finished starting attaches to the channel once it appears. A link to a game the connected library does not list returns to the library with an explanation.

An open game has the whole page under the top bar: the play page draws no heading, frame or footer, and the top bar's Games link leads back. The spending-limit dialog, the one place a game is given money, names the host the game is served from and the developer that earns its commission. Opening the wallet with `?log` in the URL adds a live developer log below the game. Each entry is numbered and shows the gap since the previous entry. Bridge requests are summarized in one line (a bet's stake, number of prizes, the most they can pay together and its exact return to player, or the amount a funding request suggests); replies are paired with their request and show the round-trip time and outcome (result, balance, rejection reason, or decline). Balance pushes, wallet progress messages, client actions and validation failures are logged with a badge, and a filter shows all events, bridge traffic only, wallet events only or errors only. Export JSON downloads the retained entries with complete payloads. It retains the latest 500 events in memory for the current view; Clear or opening another game resets it. Payload display is capped at 70,000 characters. This is a session diagnostic, not persisted wallet history, and does not capture the isolated iframe's internal console or network traffic.

Game iframes run with `sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin"`: they keep their host's origin and storage, and receive the generic atomic API, their spending limit, verified receipts and the little they need to know about the player. `wallet.hello` tells a page which methods this wallet offers and the asset it plays with, `{methods, asset: {id, symbol, decimals}, chainId}`; `wallet.info` is `{uname, alias, chainId, bankroll, recommendedStake}`: the player's uname, the alias they are shown by if they took one, and nothing else of them, their address, their channel and their balances included. The wallet listens only to the frame's window at the origin of the game's entry page and answers only that origin, so a frame that navigated elsewhere is not the game. A refusal is `{error: {code, message}}`: the code is stable and the message is for people. Questions (`wallet.hello`, `wallet.info`, `game.receipt`) are answered at once; whatever signs or asks the player takes its turn in the order the game asked, up to 32 waiting. Each game implements its own rules, sampling and persistence. The wallet checks signatures, entropy, balance changes and rejection checkpoints, without interpreting game state. Games cannot access keys, future or pending entropy, funding transactions or the wallet's storage, and cannot request arbitrary signatures. The wallet refuses a manifest or entry on its own origin, and its host sends `frame-ancestors 'none'` so wallet pages cannot be framed at all; any deployment must keep that header. Manifest URL, entry URL and developer identify the game and scope its operation IDs. Assets at those URLs are not content-pinned.

Every bridge request carries a non-negative integer ID larger than the last; any other is answered with `A request ID must be larger than the last.` The SDK counts from 1.

Only the wallet's spending-limit dialog grants spending authority. A game can ask for money (`game.requestFunds`) with a suggested amount and nothing else: every word in the dialog is the wallet's own, so no game writes into the page that authorizes it. The player sets how much of the playing balance the game may play with, or declines, and the wallet replies to the game with the decision. The amount is a number field and a slider over the same value, running linearly from nothing to the whole playing balance, a hundredth of it a step. The dialog sets the limit, so the same dialog raises it, lowers it or takes it all back; the player opens it at any time from the top bar's own button, a pending operation included: the limit signs nothing, so the playing balance it runs to is simply the signed balance less what a pending wager, payment or transfer has already committed. The limit is held only in memory for the open tab. Leaving the game, reloading or closing the tab releases it; the money never left the channel balance, and the game asks again next time. The last limit chosen for a game is kept in localStorage only as the next visit's suggestion. The play page shows no other wallet controls: the game presents its own balance, and recovering a pending operation happens through the pending banner.

The top bar carries the money on every page: the balance of the channel this tab plays with, which opens **My wallet**, and beside it the choice of what that balance is — **ETH** or **TEST**. Amounts there are truncated, never rounded, so they are a prefix of the digits shown for the same money elsewhere. An open game takes the balance's place: a player reading one figure inside the game and another above it cannot tell which money a bet is about to spend, and the figure above was never the game's to spend. What stands there instead is the authority itself — **Take money back** while the game holds money, **Give this game money** while it holds none — and both open the same dialog, the second at nothing so the amount coming back is confirmed before it moves. The asset stays named throughout, because test coins must never be mistaken for money; while a game holds money the choice is fixed until it gives it back.

Only one game per wallet can hold a limit at a time across tabs; a second tab is refused at the funding dialog. The wallet pushes the game's limit and pending state into the iframe whenever they change. The game can risk that limit, including winnings. A result recovered after a reload changes the channel balance only. Games cannot add money or alter their fee recipient. The wallet does not certify advertised rules or whole-game outcomes. See the [game SDK](../sdk/docs/game-sdk.md).

### Test coins

A channel holds one asset: the network's ETH, or test coins, the casino's own play money. Every wallet has a test channel of its own, opened at the casino with nothing deposited: nothing signed on it reaches the contract, and there is nothing on-chain to close. Test coins are signed and recorded exactly like ETH, against the casino's test bankroll: the same operations, the same receipts, the same activity history.

An account that has deposited nothing plays with test coins, and its channel is filled from the faucet the moment the wallet starts, so a game can be played at once. The faucet pays 100 coins to a test channel holding fewer than 10, as a credit the channel's own key signs. An account with an open ETH channel plays with its ETH unless the player chooses test coins. The player switches in the top bar, or in an open game's funding dialog before the game holds money: the spending limit was in the other asset, so it is released and the game is reloaded. A game needs nothing of its own for test coins; it reads the asset from `wallet.hello` and shows amounts in its symbol and decimals.

## Trust and persistence

The client ships its ABI/runtime ([client/contract-artifact.ts](../client/contract-artifact.ts)) and pins a deployment manifest independently of the casino. The manifest includes `chainId`, `contractAddress`, `operator`, `rpcUrl`, and `witnessRpcUrl` on Sepolia. It is the `deployment` field of the wallet's configuration, which the [build](#build-and-serving) publishes as `config.js`. Publish that configuration through the trusted wallet release process. A fresh browser bootstraps recovery from this manifest when the casino API is offline, returns errors or malformed data, or advertises a different chain, contract, operator or protocol. Recovery mode disables new play and cooperative closure, while preserving import/export and unilateral settlement. Import the funding key or connect its wallet, then import settlement evidence. Runtime, owner, network and RPC checks still apply. Reload after the compatible service returns.

The wallet's origin serves only the wallet. Games, and the SDK code they bundle, are served by their own hosts and run inside game iframes; the wallet's CSP restricts scripts to its own origin.

Accepted settlement state, the compact signed receipt, and clearing of pending state commit together. Invalid optional bankroll information is discarded. A bet or transfer request carries only the signed operation and the wallet's acknowledgment of its previous checkpoint. To decline, the casino signs an unchanged-balance rejection checkpoint one sequence above the operation; the wallet verifies that signature over the exact saved request, countersigns it and saves it with the rejection receipt before another operation is permitted. The countersignature travels as the acknowledgment of the next request. A timeout or generic HTTP error remains pending. Rejections consume no balance or entropy. A retry uses a new operation ID above the saved rejection, while exact retries of the rejected ID recover its original receipt. Wallet settings provide authenticated encrypted backups (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 iterations, AES-256-GCM with random salt/IV); the browser funding key, channel keys and pending operations are included. External funding keys stay in their funding wallet. No game state is included. Backup restore and funding-account selection use the same Web Lock and durable revision checks as gameplay. Restore rejects evidence downgrades, same-sequence conflicts and outstanding local intents against the latest persisted record, including changes made by another tab while decrypting. Existing close authorizations remain fenced from further play.

Recovery transactions can spend the reserved gas amount; deposits still preserve it. `claimTo` is available through the claim recipient field. Permissionless challenge/finalize/claim calls can also use a separately funded CLI relayer. Contract funding wallets must expose compatible typed-data signing and transaction submission; historical channel signing remains an EOA key.

## Build and serving

`npm run build` compiles the contract, checks it against the pinned artifact, and runs [scripts/build.ts](../scripts/build.ts), which writes the whole site to `dist/`:

| File                      | Contents                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `main.js`, `main.js.map`  | Every wallet and protocol module, bundled by esbuild from [client/main.ts](../client/main.ts) into one readable, unminified ES module        |
| `vendor/ethers.js`        | Copied byte for byte from the installed npm release of ethers (`dist/ethers.min.js`), so its hash can be compared with the published package |
| `config.js`               | `export default { network, casino, deployment? }`: this deployment's settings                                                                |
| `index.html`, `style.css` | Copied from `client/`                                                                                                                        |
| `brand/`                  | The mark                                                                                                                                     |
| `_headers`                | The response headers Cloudflare applies, from [client/\_headers](../client/_headers)                                                         |

`main.js` imports exactly `/config.js` and `/vendor/ethers.js`; `test/static.test.ts` checks that, the ethers hash and the headers. `config.js` is the JSON file named by the `HOOKEDIN_CLIENT_CONFIG` environment variable at build time, or the defaults in [client/config.ts](../client/config.ts) without it. `network` is `sepolia` or `local`, `casino` is the casino API's base URL, and `deployment` is the pinned manifest above. Settings saved in the wallet override `network` and `casino` in that browser.

`npm run build` then builds each game in [games/](../games/) into that game's own `dist/`, a separate site.

`npm run dev` builds and serves `dist/` at `http://127.0.0.1:4184` (`PORT` overrides) through [scripts/static.ts](../scripts/static.ts), which imitates the production host: it applies `_headers`, answers every extensionless route with the page, and refuses dotfiles and anything outside `dist/`. Production is a Cloudflare static-assets Worker ([wrangler.jsonc](../wrangler.jsonc)): `npm run build`, then `wrangler deploy` publishes `dist/` with the single-page fallback turned on. The headers set `script-src 'self'` and `frame-ancestors 'none'`; any other host must send the same ones.

## Casino authority and manual challenges

One casino owner controls signing and house withdrawals. Winnings are not protected from that owner or guaranteed payable. Principal protection applies to the state accepted at closure; the casino can propose an older signed state, and missing its challenge deadline can reduce the amount returned.

Check the wallet regularly while a channel is open, leaving enough time to mine a challenge within 24 hours of a closure starting. The wallet shows a persistent warning and a **Challenge stale close** button when its saved checkpoint is newer than the proposed closure. Opening the wallet or seeing the warning does not submit a transaction: the user must press the button and get the challenge mined before the deadline. Keep current evidence and ETH for gas. A watchtower is optional.

## Rejected wagers

Every bet receipt records what the player signed and what happened: the stake, the round's 64-bit outcome, what the prizes paid, the most they could have paid together and the bet's exact expected payout, so the activity list shows each bet's return to player to a hundredth of a basis point. The wallet computes these from the signed prizes, never from anything the game says.

A rejected bet keeps its receipt. A bet the casino declines on the channel's own round comes back with the round's secret: the wallet checks it against the round the bet signed and records on the receipt, at once, what the declined wager would have paid. A seat declined from a hosted round, or one the player gave up, learns it when that round is revealed: the background loop asks `GET /api/rounds/:id`, and gives up on a round the casino does not know. A run of rejections that would all have paid well is the player's evidence of selective rejection.

## Native transfers

The wallet polls for native transfers when idle, first recovering its own pending transfer or accepting one incoming credit from the developer of a game the player has funded. Unrelated senders need explicit acceptance through the wallet API; their unsolicited offers cannot occupy the channel automatically. The wallet saves the receive authorization before submission and verifies the resulting native step before crediting unallocated balance. An incoming payment never displaces a pending wager. A pending outgoing transfer shows a **Cancel transfer** action: the casino signs the rejection checkpoint above the transfer, the wallet verifies and countersigns it, the balance is unchanged and play continues at the next sequence. A payout returned by a game developer credits the channel balance; it does not raise an open game's spending limit. The transfer receipt remains usable through the existing recovery and closure paths; the game developer controls the actual payout decision.

## Compact recovery backups

Encrypted backups use `HOOKEDIN/WALLET/1` inside `HOOKEDIN/WALLET-BACKUP/1`. They include only the selected account's locally held funding key, channel keys, latest signed evidence, close authorizations, and exact unresolved operation/transaction intents. Up to 100 recent compact receipts appear once.

Backups contain no game state. They have a 16 MiB limit and depend on browser storage availability. They do not contain other accounts or a game replay archive.

A failed commit blocks further actions until durable restoration. The wallet displays the last verified observation, saved/proposed sequence, challenge deadline, pending challenge, protected remaining principal and allocated winnings. The gas reserve is a floor, not a guaranteed challenge budget.

Execution retries must repeat the original kind, amount, every prize and the developer. Both cached and pending operations reject changed terms. A game names its operations with its own IDs, which the wallet scopes by channel and game, so a game can look up an outcome after a reload with `game.receipt`. Use `wallet.getReceipt(operationId)` to retrieve a saved receipt without executing. A persistence failure blocks further wallet actions, including actions waiting on a refresh; reload from durable storage before continuing.

## Bets, and what they paid back

Every bet this wallet signed is on `/bets`, newest first: the game, the stake, what the round paid, the
result, and the return of that one bet, worked out from its own prize table before the wallet signed it.
They are added up per asset — ETH and test coins are separate channels and are never mixed into one
figure — as what those bets were **expected** to pay back and what they **did** pay back. Over a handful
of bets the second figure is luck and the first is the table, which is why both are shown.

`/games` groups the same bets by game, most staked first, with favourites above them; a favourite is a
note in this browser's localStorage and is never signed nor sent anywhere. Each game links to its public
record, `/games/<key>`, where `key` is the hash of the game's manifest URL: every bet anyone has placed in
it, read from `GET /api/games/<key>` at the casino, each naming the player's uname and no address or
channel. A game can therefore be judged on what it has actually paid, by anyone, without trusting
anything it says about itself.
